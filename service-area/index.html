Abington Township and the towns around it

Our home base is Abington Township in Montgomery County, about 15.5 square miles of neighborhoods, parks and commercial corridors. PA 611, Old York Road, runs through it, and PA 63, PA 73, PA 152 and PA 232 cross it.

The township lists these ZIP codes: 19001, 19006, 19027, 19038, 19046 and 19090. Neighborhoods and communities include Roslyn, Rydal, Meadowbrook, Ardsley, Crestmont and North Hills. Give dispatch your street address and the nearest cross street and crews will take it from there.

Reference points for giving directions

Landmarks help dispatch place you quickly. Jefferson Abington Hospital sits on Old York Road, and Holy Redeemer Hospital is in Meadowbrook on Huntingdon Pike. Penn State Abington is in Rydal, and Alverthorpe Park, Lorimer Park and the Briar Bush Nature Center are well-known green spaces.

Heavy rain also makes park and creek corridors worth watching. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address if your home sits near a stream.

Water and sewer in the township

Most Abington properties use public water from Aqua, though some use private wells. Knowing which one you have matters for a supply-line leak, because the shutoff and who to call differ. The township's sewer system covers about 250 miles of pipe and 17 pump stations.

Where your sewage goes depends on which side of the township you live on. Flow east of Edge Hill Road goes to the Pennypack interceptor of Philadelphia, the south goes to the Cheltenham interceptor, and the western third is served by the township plant on Fitzwatertown Road. That is useful when a backup affects several houses on one street, because it points to a shared main rather than one lateral. See sewage backup cleanup for the first steps.

Permits and who to ask

Building, zoning, plumbing and HVAC permits go through the Abington Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road. Electrical work is reviewed by state-licensed inspectors. A permit question usually comes up when walls, floors or wiring are being rebuilt, not during extraction and drying, so ask the office early if your repair is large.

Jenkintown and Hatboro run their own boroughs, so permit questions there go to the borough office, not the township. Each town page explains how that works.

What a call from your address looks like

Dispatch asks four things: what is wet, where the water came from, whether it is still running and whether anyone is hurt or the power is affected. Give a street address, a cross street and a note about access, such as stairs to a basement or a gated lot.

Crews bring pumps, extraction units and drying equipment. If the water came from a toilet or a sewer line, say so right away, because that changes the protective gear and the cleanup method. Storm calls often come in clusters, so say if your street has several flooded homes.

What to do before the crew arrives

Stay out of wet rooms with live power, shut off the water if you can do it safely and photograph the damage. Treat any water from a drain as contaminated. Dispatch explains the rest on the call.
