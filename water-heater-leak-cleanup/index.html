What to do when a water heater leaks

A leaking water heater is an emergency because the tank keeps draining until it is empty or the supply is closed. Our dispatch line runs 24/7, and the first steps below take only a few minutes while you wait for a crew.

Turn off the power at the breaker for an electric heater, or set a gas heater's control to off. Close the cold water shutoff valve on the pipe above the tank, which stops new water from refilling it. If the leak is small, a bucket under the drip buys time.

Hot water can scald, so keep clear of any spray. If the heater sits in a closet or basement where water has reached outlets or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.

Why water heaters fail

Most residential tanks hold about 40 to 50 gallons, and a failure can release it quickly or let it seep for weeks. Tanks corrode from the inside over time, and a slow seep at the base is often the sign of an aging tank rather than a loose fitting.

Tank failure

The steel tank corrodes through and leaks from the bottom, and the heater usually needs to be replaced.

Fitting or valve leak

Water drips from the inlet, outlet or drain valve and may be repaired by a plumber.

Temperature and pressure valve

The relief valve discharges when pressure or heat gets too high, and the pipe behind it may run to the floor.

Condensation

Moisture beads on a cold tank in humid air, which can look like a leak but is not a failure.

A plumber decides whether to repair or replace the heater. The restoration crew's job is the water on the floor and in the walls, and the two trades can work in parallel.

What cleanup after a water heater leak involves

A leak from the heater itself is usually clean supply water, but it can pass through dust and basement materials and pick up debris. Crews move quickly for that reason, and the scope often covers more than the pool visible near the heater.

  1. 1

    Extract the water

    Pumps or wet vacuums clear the floor, and drains are checked for clogs that would hold the water back.

  2. 2

    Remove wet materials

    Wet drywall at the base, insulation and cardboard storage are removed if saturated.

  3. 3

    Check hidden areas

    Meters and thermal imaging look behind walls and under finished floors adjacent to the heater.

  4. 4

    Dry and verify

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the dry standard, covered in more detail on our structural drying page.

Finished basements need extra care. Carpet and pad near the heater may be pulled, which our carpet water damage page covers, and a leak that reaches a cold floor drain is addressed on the basement water damage page.

What affects the cost

Cleanup is priced by how far the water traveled. A heater in an unfinished corner with a floor drain is a quick job, while a heater in a finished closet next to carpet or hardwood can take days.

Water volume, materials, access, drying days and rebuild scope all matter. We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call will give you a real number once you describe what is wet.

For insurance, a sudden tank failure is often covered, while a long slow seep from a worn tank may be treated as maintenance, so check your policy. Keep the failed heater or photograph the label, the fittings and the pool of water for the claim.

Replacing the heater and permits

Plumbing and fuel work is typically done under a permit. Ask the Abington Township Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road which permit your replacement needs, and have your plumber confirm. Do not run a new heater until the drying crew confirms the area is dry and the floor is safe.

Questions about water heater leak cleanup

Do you handle water heater leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes, the line is open all night and all weekend. Heaters tend to fail without warning, so call as soon as you shut the supply, and describe where the water is going.

Can I drain the rest of the tank myself?

A plumber can, using the drain valve and a hose. If the tank is leaking from the bottom, shut off the supply and power first and let a professional handle the rest.

Do I need a new water heater after a leak?

A leaking tank usually needs replacement, but a leaking fitting or valve may be repairable. A plumber can tell you which it is.

Is the water from a heater leak safe to touch?

It may be hot enough to scald, and it can pick up debris on the floor. Stay out of standing water until you know the power is off.
