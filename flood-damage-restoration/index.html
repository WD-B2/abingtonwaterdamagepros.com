Flood water is an emergency: what to do first

Flood water is an emergency because it is rarely clean and it keeps rising until the weather or the ground changes. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call as soon as you can do so safely. Until a crew reaches you, the priority is people, power and photographs.

Leave any room with standing water if electricity might be involved, and call the utility or 911 about downed lines or gas smells. Do not use household appliances or vacuums on wet floors. Photograph or record the water line on walls and furniture before anything moves.

Whole-house flooding calls often start with water entering from several points at once. That is why a flood job begins with safety and sorting, and extraction comes after.

Why flood water is treated differently

Flood water counts as Category 3 water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard when it rises from the ground, a creek or a storm sewer, because it can carry sewage, chemicals and soil. Porous materials that soak it up, such as carpet, pad, insulation and drywall, are usually removed rather than dried.

Hard surfaces can often be cleaned and sanitized, and framing can be dried and treated. A crew uses protective gear and may set containment to keep contamination from spreading into dry rooms.

That is also why the water categories matter for cost. Clean water from a pipe is cheaper to handle than contaminated flood water, and the scope we write up explains which category applies to yours.

Where flooding comes from around Abington

Heavy rain affects this part of Montgomery County through creeks and storm drains. When the remnants of Ida crossed the region on September 1, 2021, the National Weather Service reported major flooding on Wissahickon Creek at Fort Washington and flooding along Pennypack Creek, which shows how quickly creek levels can rise after a long rain.

Flood exposure is address specific. To see whether your property sits in a mapped flood zone, check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, and ask the Abington Township Community Development Department how floodplain rules affect repairs. The township's planning documents describe a Sandy Run floodplain corridor through Crestmont, which our Crestmont page explains.

Whole-house flooding and crawl spaces

When water reaches several rooms or fills a crawl space, the sequence still starts with extraction, but the drying plan gets bigger. Crawl spaces need their own treatment for insulation, vapor barriers and wood framing, which our crawl space water damage page covers.

For a flood that is mostly below grade, the basement water damage page explains pumps, sump failures and finished rooms. If the water came from the sewer line rather than the creek, see sewage backup cleanup.

Flood insurance is separate

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood damage, and flood coverage is a separate policy. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department tells policyholders to ask whether floods are covered, photograph damage before cleanup and contact the insurer promptly. Read your policy or call your agent before you assume anything.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and the written scope lists rooms, materials and water category so an adjuster can follow it. Call for a real number once you can describe the water.

Questions about flood damage cleanup

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call, say how deep the water is and whether utilities are affected, and dispatch will go through safety steps. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Can carpet and drywall be saved after a flood?

Usually not when the water is Category 3 flood water. Porous materials that absorbed it are generally removed, while hard surfaces and framing can often be cleaned and dried.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Standard policies usually exclude flooding, and separate flood coverage is needed. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

How do I find out whether my address is in a flood zone?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and enter your address. The township's building and zoning staff can explain how that affects repair permits.
