Water damage in Crestmont homes

Crestmont is an established community in Abington Township, located around the intersection of Easton Road and Old Welsh Road and southwest of Willow Grove. Its mailing ZIP code is 19090. The Crestmont station on SEPTA's Warminster Line serves it, and Crestmont Park, a 19.4-acre township park at Old Welsh Road and Reservoir Avenue, sits across from Willow Grove Park Mall.

Those landmarks help dispatch place your call quickly. When you phone about water in the house, give the street and the nearest cross street, and mention if you are near the park or the station.

A creek corridor and what it means

Abington Township's planning documents say the Sandy Run floodplain extends from Roy Chester Park through Crestmont and Roslyn, and that parts are preserved as open space in Grove Park and Roslyn Park. The same documents describe flooding along that corridor in recent decades. A separate township project at Roychester and Grove Parks aims to stabilize stream banks and improve how flood water soaks in.

This is context for the corridor, not a flood rating for your street. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here, so look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Our flood damage cleanup page explains what a crew does when water rises into a home.

Water damage that has nothing to do with the creek

Most homes on a typical street here will never see creek water, and still have water damage. A burst supply line, a water heater that fails, a refrigerator ice-maker line or a clogged drain can put water through a floor in minutes.

The right page depends on the source. A tank failure is covered by water heater leak cleanup, a hose or valve failure by appliance leak water damage, and water that arrives below grade by basement water damage cleanup.

What to do before help arrives

  1. 1

    Stop the source

    Close the main shutoff or the fixture valve if you can reach it without stepping into water.

  2. 2

    Stay clear of power

    Keep out of rooms where water meets outlets, and call the utility or 911 for live wires.

  3. 3

    Photograph first

    Record the damage and the source before moving belongings.

  4. 4

    Lift what you can

    Move small items and papers to a dry room if it is safe to do so.

  5. 5

    Call

    Describe the water, and ask what to do until a crew starts.

If the water came from a sewer line or a toilet, leave it alone and wait for a crew, because that water can carry contamination.

Extraction, drying and repair

A crew pumps or extracts standing water, removes materials that cannot be saved and sets air movers and dehumidifiers. Moisture readings show where water traveled, including behind walls and under floors, and the drying continues until readings reach a dry reference, following the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

Repairs come after the structure is dry. Drywall, trim, flooring and paint are restored once the cause is fixed. Our structural drying page explains the equipment in more detail.

Utilities and permits

Most of Abington Township receives public water through Aqua Pennsylvania and public sewer service from the township's wastewater department. If water entered through a sewer line, tell dispatch, because the cleanup method changes.

Building, zoning and plumbing permits go through the township's Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road. Ask the office early if the repair will open walls or change plumbing.

Insurance in plain terms

Insurance coverage depends on the cause of the loss and your policy. Photos, dated notes and a written scope from the crew help describe what happened and what work is proposed. They do not guarantee that a claim will be approved, and flood damage is usually covered only by a separate flood policy.

Questions from Crestmont homeowners

What ZIP code does Crestmont use?

Crestmont uses 19090, the Willow Grove ZIP code, though the community is in Abington Township.

Is every Crestmont street in a floodplain?

No. The township describes a floodplain corridor along Sandy Run, not the whole community, so check your own address on the FEMA map.

Do you only handle flood water?

No. Crews handle burst pipes, appliance leaks, water heater failures, sewage backups and storm intrusion, as well as drying and repair.

Should I wait for my insurer before calling?

Call your insurer promptly, and do not wait to stop the water. Take only the emergency steps needed to prevent further damage.
