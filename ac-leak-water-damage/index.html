Where air conditioner water comes from

An air conditioner pulls moisture out of indoor air, and that water is meant to drain away through a condensate line. When the line clogs, the drain pan cracks or overflows, or the evaporator coil freezes and then thaws, the water ends up inside the house instead.

Air handlers often sit in attics, closets and basements, so the water may run along joists, through a ceiling or into a closet carpet before it is seen. Callers often describe a damp closet, a stain near a vent or a floor that has started to cup, and each points back to the same small line.

Turn the system off at the thermostat the moment you see water so it stops making more.

Why AC leaks stay hidden

Slow drip

Condensate overflows a little at a time, so the damage grows for days before a stain shows.

Hidden location

Attics and closets are rarely checked, and insulation hides the water.

Seasonal timing

The leak starts when the system first runs in warm weather, often when no one is watching it.

Frozen coil

Ice on the coil melts when the system is shut off, and the pan cannot hold the meltwater.

A clogged condensate line is the usual cause, and an HVAC technician can clear it and install a float switch that shuts the system off if water backs up. The restoration crew handles the water and the damage it left.

Drying and repair after an AC leak

Because the water is clean at the start but sits in insulation and drywall for days, drying has to reach the framing and not just the surface. Crews use moisture meters and thermal imaging, then open the ceiling or wall where readings stay high.

  1. 1

    Stop the leak

    The system is off and the line, pan or coil problem is repaired by an HVAC technician.

  2. 2

    Find the wet area

    Meters and thermal imaging trace water through insulation, drywall and framing.

  3. 3

    Remove saturated materials

    Wet insulation, soft drywall and damp carpet pad are removed.

  4. 4

    Dry the space

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference materials.

  5. 5

    Repair

    Ceiling, wall and flooring are patched and finished.

If the water has run long enough to stain or sag the ceiling, our ceiling water damage repair page explains what that repair involves. When water has been in a closet or under carpet for days, the mold remediation page explains how crews decide whether mold work is needed.

How the estimate is set

An AC leak that is caught early is small, and one that ran for weeks is larger. The estimate depends on how many rooms are affected, which materials are wet, how many days of drying the structure needs and how much rebuilding follows.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the simplest way to get a number for your property. Insurance may cover a sudden failure but not wear or neglect, so check your policy and ask your insurer, and keep photos of the line, the pan and the damage.

Floors that cup after a leak

Wood flooring takes up water slowly and shows cupping, which is the edges rising, days after the leak starts. If your flooring has changed shape near an air handler or a vent, see our hardwood floor water damage page for how crews dry wood floors and when sanding is possible.

What homeowners can do before the crew arrives

Place a bucket or towels under drips and move stored boxes out of the closet or attic space. Do not run the system again until the cause is fixed. Open the area to air if weather allows, and photograph the water and the equipment label.

Before the crew arrives

Turn the air conditioner off at the thermostat so it stops producing water. Do not touch wet ceiling light fixtures or outlets, and stay out of a room where the ceiling is sagging.

  • Place a bucket under active drips and move furniture out of the wet area.
  • Photograph the stain, the drain pan and the unit before cleanup.
  • Do not poke a bulging ceiling unless water is actively pooling and you can do it from a safe angle.

Crews dry the structure following the drying principles in ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

Questions about AC leak water damage

Why is my air conditioner leaking water inside?

The most common cause is a clogged condensate drain line, followed by a cracked or overflowing drain pan and a frozen evaporator coil. An HVAC technician can find out which one it is.

Is mold a risk after an AC leak?

Mold needs moisture and time, and a leak that runs into insulation or a closet can supply both. Dry the area quickly and ask a crew to check for growth if the area stays damp.

Can I pour bleach down the condensate line?

Many HVAC technicians use other cleaners and a wet vacuum, and bleach can damage some parts. Ask your technician what suits your system.

Will insurance cover an AC leak?

A sudden failure is often covered, while gradual leaks from poor maintenance often are not. Check your policy.
