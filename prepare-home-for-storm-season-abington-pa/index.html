How to Prepare Your Abington, PA Home for Storm Season

Most storm water damage starts with something small, like a clogged gutter or a sump pump that fails. These steps cost little and shorten the cleanup if water does come in.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

24/7 emergency line

(445) 263-2078Call now

The short answer

Before heavy weather, clear gutters and downspouts, test the sump pump, check the roof and flashing, learn whether your address is on a flood map and review your insurance. Take photos of your home and belongings now, and keep supplies ready. If you want flood insurance, FEMA notes there is typically a 30-day waiting period before a policy takes effect, so buy early.

None of this prevents all damage, but it reduces the chance and speeds the cleanup.

Water enters in a handful of ways

Storm water finds the weakest point in a home, and the points are predictable.

Where storm water gets in and what to do before the weather

A simple pre-storm checklist

  1. 1

    Clear the gutters

    Remove leaves and check that downspouts drain away from the house.

  2. 2

    Test the sump

    Pour water in the pit, confirm the pump starts and check the discharge.

  3. 3

    Walk the roof line

    Look from the ground for missing shingles, and check the attic for stains afterward.

  4. 4

    Move valuables

    Keep important items off basement floors and in waterproof containers.

  5. 5

    Charge and stock

    Keep phones charged, flashlights ready and the water main location known.

Know where the main water valve and the electrical panel are, and make sure everyone in the house does too. In an emergency, minutes matter.

Check your flood risk and coverage

Flood risk is address specific. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your property, and ask your township or borough how floodplain rules apply. Homeowners policies usually exclude flood, and flood insurance is a separate policy.

If you are not sure what your insurance covers, call your agent before storm season. Ask about water backup coverage, which many policies exclude without an endorsement.

Local weather and flood context

The National Weather Service's review of the remnants of Ida on September 1, 2021 recorded flooding on Pennypack Creek and major flooding on Wissahickon Creek at Fort Washington, showing how quickly creeks in this part of Montgomery County can rise after a long rain. FEMA says flood policies typically carry a 30-day waiting period from the date of purchase, so a policy bought during a storm warning may not help.

Document your home now

A photo record takes twenty minutes and helps for years. Walk each room with a phone and record walls, ceilings, floors, appliances and valuables. Store the files somewhere that does not depend on your house, such as cloud storage or an email to yourself.

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises policyholders to photograph or record damage and to keep receipts and records, and a baseline set of photos makes it easier to show what changed.

What to do during and after a storm

During the storm, stay safe and out of flooded areas, and avoid driving through water. After it, check for leaks from a safe position and photograph anything you find. If water has entered, start with the steps on our water extraction page, and if it came from a creek or the ground, see the flood damage cleanup page.

Never wade into water that could touch electrical equipment. Call the utility or 911 for downed lines or gas smells, and then call the 24/7 line.

Supplies worth having

Sump pump backup

A battery backup keeps the pump running through an outage.

Wet vacuum

A shop vacuum handles small amounts of water on hard floors.

Tarps and sandbags

Temporary protection for openings or door thresholds.

Flashlights and batteries

Light for dark basements and outages.

A battery backup is not a promise against flooding, but it reduces one of the most common failure points.

Where the guidance comes from

For flood readiness, the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and the National Weather Service are the references to check. For cleanup after a storm, the published standard is ANSI/IICRC S500, which classifies water by source and sets the drying approach.

A simple pre-storm walk-through

Do a short walk-through before heavy weather arrives. Check that downspouts discharge away from the foundation, that gutters are clear and that window wells are free of leaves. Look at the ceiling in the attic or top floor for stains that suggest a roof problem.

Know where your main water shutoff and electrical panel are, and make sure everyone in the house can find them. If you have a sump pump, test it by pouring water into the pit and confirming that it runs and drains. A pump that fails during a storm is a common cause of basement water.

Questions about storm preparation in Abington

When should I buy flood insurance?

Early. FEMA says there is typically a 30-day waiting period from purchase before a policy takes effect.

How do I know if my home is in a flood zone?

Look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask your municipality how it applies floodplain rules.

Does my homeowners policy cover storm damage?

Wind and rain damage is often covered, while flooding usually is not. Read your policy.

Should I test my sump pump?

Yes, before heavy weather. Pour water into the pit and make sure the pump starts and discharges away from the house.
