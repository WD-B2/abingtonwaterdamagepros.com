What kitchen water damage looks like

Kitchens leak in predictable places. A sink supply line drips, a drain trap loosens, a dishwasher hose splits or a faucet seal fails, and the water pools inside a cabinet that was never meant to get wet.

Callers often describe a burst pipe under the sink with water spreading over the kitchen floor, or a "water lake" that came from somewhere unseen. Close the stop valves under the sink or the main to stop it.

Then empty the cabinet, take photographs and keep the failed part for your insurer.

Cabinets, counters and the floor under them

Cabinet material decides the damage. Solid wood cabinets can often be dried, while particleboard and MDF swell and crumble when they soak water, and the toe-kick and cabinet base are the first places to fail.

How kitchen materials respond to water
MaterialWhat happensUsual outcome
Solid wood cabinetsSwell slowly, can dry if caught earlyOften dried and refinished
Particleboard and MDF cabinetsSwell, crumble and lose strengthOften replaced at the damaged section
Laminate countertopsEdges lift where seams get wetReplace at the seam if lifted
Stone or solid-surface topsNot affected by water but may need unmountingLifted for access, then reinstalled
Vinyl, tile and laminate floorsWater gets under seams and into the subfloorDry under the floor, replace if lifted

How crews dry a kitchen

The kitchen is crowded, so crews often remove the toe-kick panel, drill small access holes and push air under the cabinets. This dries the space without tearing out the whole kitchen, which saves cost when the cabinets can be saved.

  1. 1

    Extract

    Wet vacuums remove water from the floor and from inside the cabinets.

  2. 2

    Open the cabinet base

    Toe-kicks and bottom panels come off so air can reach the wet cavity.

  3. 3

    Map moisture

    Meters check cabinets, the subfloor and the wall behind.

  4. 4

    Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference material.

  5. 5

    Repair

    Damaged cabinets, flooring and drywall are replaced or refinished.

Water that spreads from a dishwasher is covered on our appliance leak water damage page, and our hardwood floor water damage page explains what happens when the water gets under wood floors.

Why kitchen mold shows up fast

Warm, damp, closed spaces under sinks are ideal for growth once moisture sits. If the cabinet smells musty days after a leak or has dark spots, the mold remediation page explains how crews inspect and handle it.

Drying early is the best way to avoid that step.

What affects the cost

Kitchen price depends on how many cabinets are wet, what they are made of, the flooring, how far the water spread, how many days of drying are needed and how much rebuilding follows. A single base cabinet is a smaller job than a floor-to-ceiling kitchen.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to a real number. A sudden leak is often covered by homeowners insurance, while a slow leak under a sink may not be. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

Kitchens in older homes and nearby boroughs

Older kitchens may have plaster walls, old supply lines and layered flooring, so repair takes more care. The pages on Jenkintown and Hatboro describe the places we serve near the township.

Safety steps in a wet kitchen

Shut off the supply to the sink, dishwasher or refrigerator line that is leaking. Keep appliances unplugged or powered off at the panel if water has reached outlets.

  • Do not eat food that sat in floodwater.
  • Empty the cabinet under the sink and photograph it.
  • Do not run a dishwasher or disposal until the leak is fixed.
  • Keep the failed part for your insurer.

Cabinet and subfloor drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500.

Questions about kitchen water damage

Can wet kitchen cabinets be saved?

Solid wood cabinets often can be dried if caught early, while particleboard cabinets that swell usually need replacing. A crew can check with a meter.

Do you have to tear out the whole kitchen?

Not usually. Crews often open the toe-kick and dry under and behind cabinets, and remove only what is too damaged.

What if the kitchen wall is damaged from outside?

Water from siding or windows can reach a kitchen wall, and the source needs repair first. A crew can dry the wall once the outside is sealed.

Will insurance cover kitchen water damage?

Sudden failures are often covered, but slow leaks often are not. Check your policy.
