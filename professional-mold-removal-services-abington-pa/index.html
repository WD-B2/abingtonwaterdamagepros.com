Professional Mold Removal in Abington, PA: What to Ask First

Mold removal bids can look alike and still cover very different work. These are the questions that separate a clear scope from a vague one.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

24/7 emergency line

(445) 263-2078Call now

The short answer

Ask every company for a written scope that names the areas, the materials to be removed, the containment method, the standard its crews follow and how the work will be verified. Ask whether it addresses the moisture source, whether it uses independent clearance testing and what is excluded. Prices that arrive without that detail are hard to compare.

This guide is for people choosing a company, not for people trying to diagnose mold. If you want to know how remediation itself works, our mold remediation page walks through the process.

Why mold bids differ so much

Two companies can look at the same bathroom ceiling and write very different bids. One includes containment, removal of the damaged drywall, cleaning of the framing and a clearance step. Another quotes only a surface cleaning and a sealer.

The gap is not always about quality. It is about scope, and scope is what you are buying. A written scope makes the gap visible, and the questions below pull it out.

Questions to ask before you hire

Questions to put in front of every mold company
QuestionWhat a clear answer sounds like
What standard do your crews follow?ANSI/IICRC S520 is the published standard for mold remediation
What exactly is included?Areas, materials removed, containment, cleaning, disposal, drying and rebuild
How do you handle the moisture source?It is fixed or referred to the right trade before work, because growth returns without it
How is the work verified?Visual verification, or independent clearance testing by a third party
What is excluded?Rebuild, painting, plumbing, roof or HVAC repairs, and testing if separate
How is the price set?Written scope with line items and a plan for changes

The S520 question is a good place to start. The standard is real and published, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. A company that cannot explain how it follows a standard is not a good sign.

What to look for in the written scope

A scope should read like a plan. Look for these items.

Containment

Plastic barriers, negative air and protection of vents and surfaces.

Removal

Which materials come out, such as drywall, carpet and insulation, and how they are disposed of.

Cleaning

HEPA vacuuming and damp wiping of framing and surfaces.

Drying

How the area will be dried and how moisture will be checked.

Verification

How and by whom the work will be checked once it is done.

If a quote says only "mold treatment," ask what that means. Surface treatments over wet material do not fix the underlying issue.

Independent clearance testing

Some people ask for independent clearance testing, which means a third party checks the area after remediation. The tester should be separate from the contractor to avoid a conflict of interest. Clearance testing is not required for every job, and it adds cost, but it provides a record that can help with a home sale or a lender's questions.

We cannot promise any buyer, lender or insurer will accept a given result. Ask them what documentation they need before work starts, so the testing matches their requirement.

Licensing and credentials

State rules on mold work vary, so ask whether your state licenses mold work and ask each company to explain its credentials in writing. Do not accept a verbal answer. Pennsylvania's home improvement rules also apply to many contractors, and the box below summarizes them.

What Pennsylvania's Attorney General says about contractors

Under Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act, contractors must register with the Attorney General, and the registration number, which starts with "PA" followed by digits, must appear in ads, contracts and estimates. You can verify a contractor at 1-888-520-6680. Work over $500 requires a written, signed contract that includes a description of the work, start and completion dates, the total price and a cancellation notice.

Red flags that matter

  1. 1

    No written scope

    The company will not put what it will do in writing.

  2. 2

    Pressure to sign

    The price disappears if you wait a day.

  3. 3

    Surface-only work

    The bid ignores the moisture source and the wet materials.

  4. 4

    Vague about standards

    The company cannot say what its crews follow.

  5. 5

    Promises of permanence

    A promise that mold is gone for good is overpromising.

Doing the water work first

Mold work depends on drying. If a leak caused the growth, the water has to be stopped and the structure dried, or the work will be undone. Our structural drying page explains how readings show the space is dry, and the water extraction page explains the first removal step after a leak.

A closet or a ceiling in a finished basement often has a simple water source, such as an air conditioner line or an upstairs toilet. The AC leak water damage page covers the first, and the ceiling water damage repair page covers the second.

Getting more than one scope

Get at least two written scopes, and compare them line by line, not by total. If one is much lower, ask what it leaves out. A bid that omits containment or clearance may look cheaper and cost more later.

Questions about hiring mold removal in Abington

Does my insurance pay for mold removal?

It depends on the cause and your policy. Mold that follows a sudden covered leak may be treated differently from mold caused by long-term moisture, so ask your insurer.

Is independent clearance testing required?

Not usually, but it can provide a record for a home sale or loan. Ask the buyer or lender what they need.

How do I know a company follows S520?

Ask them to explain how their process matches it, and get the scope in writing. The standard is published, so a good answer should be specific.

Can I compare quotes by price alone?

No. Compare scopes line by line, because the price depends on what is included.
