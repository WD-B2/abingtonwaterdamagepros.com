How to Choose a Water Damage Restoration Company in Abington, PA

Water damage forces fast decisions, which is when mistakes happen. This checklist helps you compare companies on registration, scope, standards and contract terms.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

24/7 emergency line

(445) 263-2078Call now

The short answer

Choose a company that will put its scope and price in writing before work begins, can show its Pennsylvania contractor registration number, explains the industry standard its crews follow and does not pressure you to sign. Compare at least two written scopes line by line, and verify any contractor through the Attorney General.

This guide is for buyers, and it applies to any company you consider, including us. Ask whichever company you hire to put its details in writing.

Start with Pennsylvania's contractor rules

Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act sets baseline rules for many home repair contractors. Know what to ask for.

What the Attorney General says

Contractors must register with the Pennsylvania Attorney General, and the registration number, which begins with "PA" followed by digits, must appear in ads, contracts and estimates. You can verify a contractor by calling 1-888-520-6680. A job over $500 needs a written, signed contract that lists the work, the start and completion dates, the total price and a cancellation notice. On a time-and-materials contract, the total cannot exceed the written estimate by more than 10 percent without a signed change order.

What those rules give you

Those items give you a basic checklist. A contractor that cannot supply a registration number, a written contract or an estimate is a risk.

The questions that separate companies

What to ask and what to listen for
QuestionA good answer
Can I see your Pennsylvania registration number?A "PA" number on the estimate that checks out with the Attorney General
Can I have the scope and price in writing before work starts?Yes, with rooms, materials, equipment and drying plan
What standard do your crews follow?ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage, S520 for mold
How will you document the damage?Photos, moisture readings and logs you can give your insurer
What is excluded?A clear list, such as rebuild, plumbing or roof work
How do changes get approved?A signed change order, as the law requires

The S500 question is useful because the standard is published. Ask the company to describe how its process matches it, and listen for specifics such as moisture mapping, drying equipment and dry-standard readings.

What a good written scope contains

A scope is the heart of the comparison, and it should describe the work and not just the total.

Rooms and materials

Which rooms, floors, walls and ceilings are included, and what comes out.

Water category

Whether it is clean, gray or contaminated, since that affects the work.

Equipment and days

What drying equipment is used and how it is monitored.

Exclusions

What is not included, such as rebuilding or plumbing repairs.

Prices without scopes cannot be compared. Our structural drying page explains what drying work involves, and the water extraction page covers the first stage.

Insurance, direct billing and approvals

Be cautious about promises regarding insurance. No company can promise that your insurer will approve a claim, and any claim of "direct billing" or "insurance approved" should be checked with your insurer. Ask your insurer whether it has a required contractor process, and make sure you understand what you are signing before you give anyone authority to deal with your insurer.

A company can help with documentation, and that is different from promising a payout. Our burst pipe water damage page shows what a mitigation record looks like.

Red flags

  1. 1

    Pressure to sign

    The offer expires within hours or the company asks for a large payment upfront.

  2. 2

    No written scope

    The company gives only a verbal price.

  3. 3

    No registration

    The company cannot supply a Pennsylvania registration number.

  4. 4

    Vague about standards

    The company cannot explain how it dries a structure.

  5. 5

    Door knocking after a storm

    Unsolicited offers after a storm deserve extra checking.

Timing matters, but so does checking

Water damage needs fast action, so you may not have days to research. Do the checks in the first hour. Ask for the registration number, ask for the scope in writing and ask for the standard. Those three questions take a few minutes and filter out many problems.

If the first company cannot answer, call a second. A good company will not object to being checked.

Local knowledge

A company that knows the area can help with permits and local conditions. Abington Township's Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road handles building, zoning, plumbing and HVAC permits, and a contractor should be able to say which of their repairs need one.

For creek-side and compact-borough conditions, see our pages on Hatboro and Jenkintown.

What we can and cannot say about ourselves

We take 24/7 calls and send licensed and insured professionals, and we explain the scope and the price before work starts. We do not promise insurance approval or exact arrival times. Ask for details in writing, whichever company you choose.

Questions to ask on the first call

Good questions are specific and easy to answer. Ask what the company will do in the first hour, how it decides what to dry and what to remove, and how it records moisture readings. A company that works carefully can explain its process in plain words.

Ask how the estimate is built too. The price should follow from a written scope that lists rooms, materials and equipment, not from a number quoted before anyone has seen the damage. If a figure changes, ask for the reason in writing before work continues.

Finally, ask who stays on the job. Water damage involves crews, a project lead and sometimes subcontractors for repairs, and you should know who to call with questions.

Questions about choosing a restoration company in Abington

How do I verify a Pennsylvania contractor?

Check the "PA" registration number on the estimate with the Attorney General at 1-888-520-6680.

Should I accept the first company that calls?

Not without a written scope and a registration number. A few minutes of checking is worth it.

What does S500 mean?

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration. Ask any company how its process follows it.

Can a company promise my insurer will pay?

No. Coverage depends on your policy and your insurer.
