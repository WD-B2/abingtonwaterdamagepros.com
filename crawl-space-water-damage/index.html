Crawl Space Water Damage in Abington, PA

Water under the house usually has a source

A crawl space is the shallow void under a floor, and when it holds water the source is usually one of four things. Groundwater can seep in after heavy rain, a plumbing line can leak, a broken irrigation or outdoor line can send water in, or humid air can condense on cool surfaces and pool.

This page covers the damage from any of them. Whether crawl spaces are common in a given neighborhood depends on the house, and this page does not claim a local rate. If your home has one, treat any water in it as something to deal with soon.

Callers often tell us they found water in the crawl space after a broken irrigation backflow or a plumbing failure. Locating and stopping the source comes first, then pumping.

What water does under a floor

Water in a crawl space wets the underside of the subfloor, the joists, the insulation and the vapor barrier. Because the space is dark and poorly ventilated, it dries slowly and stays damp.

Joists and subfloor

Wood absorbs moisture and can swell, soften or stain if it stays wet.

Insulation

Fiberglass between joists holds water and sags, which leaves the floor above colder and wet.

Vapor barrier

A torn or displaced plastic barrier lets ground moisture rise into the space.

Ducts and wiring

Wet ductwork and exposed wiring need inspection before the system runs again.

Floors above

Hardwood, carpet and tile above can cup, buckle or stain from the moisture below.

How crews work in a crawl space

Access is tight, and crews work in protective suits with light and ventilation. They pump standing water, remove wet insulation and any trash left behind, and check the moisture level in the framing.

  1. 1

    Find and stop the source

    A plumber or the owner fixes the leak, closes the line or addresses seepage.

  2. 2

    Pump out water

    Small pumps and wet vacuums remove standing water and mud.

  3. 3

    Remove saturated insulation

    Wet fiberglass and debris are bagged and taken out.

  4. 4

    Dry the framing

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until joist readings reach the dry standard.

  5. 5

    Check for growth

    Wood is inspected, and mold work is handled by crews following the S520 standard if needed.

Our structural drying page explains the equipment and the readings, and the mold remediation page explains what happens if growth is found on the framing.

Drying versus fixing the crawl space

Drying removes the water that is there now, but it does not stop more from entering. If groundwater is the cause, a drainage or encapsulation contractor may be the right next call, and we say so honestly. Restoration is not waterproofing.

Encapsulation, which seals the ground and walls with a heavy vapor barrier, is a separate project. Crawl space insulation and vapor barriers are often replaced after drying, and your contractor can tell you what suits your house.

What affects the cost

Cost depends on how much water there is, how hard the space is to enter, how much insulation and wood is wet, and how many days of drying are needed. Some spaces need only pumping and a few days of airflow, and others need removal of insulation and repair of the barrier.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest route to a real number. Insurance often treats a sudden break differently from seepage that builds over time, so read your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph the space before cleanup.

When flooding fills the crawl space

Flood water entering a crawl space follows different rules because of contamination. See our flood damage cleanup page, and for your borough, the pages on Jenkintown and Hatboro.

Safety before anyone goes under the house

Do not enter a flooded crawl space. Standing water can hide electrical hazards, and tight spaces carry other risks, so leave entry to a crew with the right equipment.

  • Shut off the water supply if a pipe is the source.
  • Do not run wet-area electrical equipment from an extension cord.
  • Photograph visible water from the access hatch only.
  • Mention any musty smell or visible mold when you call.

Drying work follows ANSI/IICRC S500, and mold work follows ANSI/IICRC S520.

Questions about crawl space water damage

Is it safe to go into a crawl space with standing water?

Usually not by yourself. Water can hide wiring, ducts and sharp debris, and the space has limited air and light. Call for a crew with the right gear.

Can a crawl space dry on its own?

Rarely. The space is dark and enclosed, so moisture lingers, and wet insulation holds water against the floor above.

Is crawl space water damage the same as a flooded basement?

The cleanup steps are similar, but the access, the insulation and the framing exposure are different. See the basement page if your water is in a finished lower level.

Will insurance cover crawl space water?

Coverage depends on the source. A sudden pipe break is often covered, while seepage from groundwater usually needs separate coverage. Ask your insurer.
