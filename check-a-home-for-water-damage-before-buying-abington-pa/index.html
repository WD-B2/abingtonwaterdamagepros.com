How to Check a Home for Water Damage Before Buying in Abington, PA

Water damage is one of the costliest surprises in a home purchase. This checklist shows what to look for at a showing and what to ask before you sign.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

(445) 263-2078Call now

The short answer

Look for stains, soft spots, musty smells and signs of fresh paint in ceilings, walls and basements, and ask for the seller's disclosure form and repair records. Pennsylvania sellers must disclose known material defects, and the form asks about basements and crawl spaces, water and sewage and the roof. Hire a home inspector, and consider a moisture meter or a thermal camera check if anything looks suspicious.

A showing is short, so know where water damage hides. This guide covers what to look for.

What to look for room by room

Water leaves marks, and some are subtle. Look in the places where leaks collect.

Water damage signs and where to look
WhereWhat to look forPossible meaning
CeilingsStains with brown edges, bubbling paint, saggingRoof, plumbing or HVAC leak above
WallsPeeling paint, soft drywall, wavy surfaceLeak inside the wall or moisture behind it
FloorsCupping, buckling, warped boards, stained carpetMoisture from below or a past leak
BasementWhite powder on walls, rust on the base of the furnace or water heaterSeepage or repeated wetting
Under sinksStains, swollen cabinet bases, corrosionSlow drips
WindowsStained sills, peeling trimSeal or flashing leaks
SmellMusty odor, especially in closets and basementsHidden moisture

A fresh coat of paint on one patch of ceiling is a clue. Ask why it was painted.

Use the disclosure form

Pennsylvania's Real Estate Seller Disclosure Law requires sellers to disclose known material defects, and the standard form covers basements and crawl spaces, water and sewage systems and the roof. Read each answer, and compare it with what you see. A "no known problems" answer next to a freshly repainted basement is a prompt to ask questions.

The law does not require sellers to investigate, so it only covers what they know. That is why the inspection matters.

Ask these questions

  1. 1

    Has there been a leak or flood?

    Ask when, where, what caused it and what was done.

  2. 2

    Are there repair records?

    Ask for invoices, permits and moisture readings from the repair.

  3. 3

    Was there an insurance claim?

    Ask whether a claim was filed and for what.

  4. 4

    Does the basement take on water?

    Ask about the sump pump, drains and any past seepage.

  5. 5

    Why was this area repainted or replaced?

    Ask what was covered and when.

Written answers are better than verbal ones. If the seller cannot answer, an inspection can fill the gap.

Inspections and when to dig deeper

A general inspection catches visible problems. For deeper checks, ask the inspector or a restoration crew to use a moisture meter and a thermal camera, which show wet materials behind surfaces. If readings are high, ask for a written scope of what it would take to dry and repair the area.

Our structural drying page explains how moisture readings work, and the wall and drywall water damage repair page explains what stains and soft spots can mean.

Mold during a purchase

If an inspector finds mold, ask where the moisture came from before you ask how to remove it. Growth with an active leak is a different problem from an old stain on a dry surface. A lender may require remediation before funding, and independent clearance testing can provide a record after the work. Our mold remediation page explains how the process works.

We cannot tell you whether a lender or buyer will accept a given result, so ask yours what documentation it needs.

Permits and records for local homes

Past repairs may have been permitted. Abington Township's Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road handles building, zoning, plumbing and HVAC permits, and Jenkintown's Borough Office at 700 Summit Avenue handles permits in the borough. Asking the office that covers the address whether permits exist for a major repair can help confirm what was done. Rules vary, so ask what records are available.

Flood risk and insurance before you buy

Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for the address, and ask insurers about coverage and cost before you commit. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, and FEMA notes a flood policy typically has a 30-day waiting period, so plan the timing around closing.

If the home is near a creek or in a low-lying area, ask about past flooding and about the sump system. Our flood damage cleanup page explains how flood water is handled if it happens after you move in.

Negotiating when you find damage

If you find water damage, you can ask the seller to repair it, to credit you or to reduce the price, or you can walk away. A written estimate for drying and repair gives you a number to negotiate with. Talk with your agent about contingencies and timelines.

Do not ignore small signs. A stain on a basement wall can be a symptom of a larger issue, and our basement water damage page explains what below-grade water can do.

Checking homes in nearby boroughs

Compact boroughs and creek-side towns each have their own patterns. If you are buying in Jenkintown or Hatboro, see our pages on water damage restoration in Jenkintown and Hatboro.

Questions about buying a home with possible water damage

Can a seller hide water damage in Pennsylvania?

The disclosure law requires sellers to disclose known material defects and not to make misleading statements. It does not require them to investigate, so an inspection matters.

What is the best way to find hidden water damage?

A moisture meter and a thermal camera show wet materials behind surfaces. Ask your inspector to use them if anything looks suspicious.

Should I buy a home with a wet basement?

It depends on the source and the cost to fix. Ask for repair records and a written estimate before you decide.

Does insurance cover a home I have not closed on?

No. Check coverage and flood options before closing, and ask about waiting periods.
