Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Abington, PA?

Coverage depends on what caused the water, how fast it happened and what your policy says. Here is how to read the difference before you file.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

(445) 263-2078Call now

The short answer

Homeowners insurance often covers water damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or an appliance hose that splits. It often does not cover damage from slow leaks, long-term neglect or flooding, and flood damage usually needs a separate flood policy.

That is a general pattern and not a promise. Policies differ in wording, endorsements and deductibles, so the only reliable answer comes from reading your own policy and asking your insurer. Nothing on this page says that a claim will be approved or what any insurer will pay.

Why the cause matters more than the water

Insurers care about how the water got in. Two homes can have the same wet carpet and get different answers, because one lost water to a supply line that failed this morning and the other had a drip under a sink for six months.

Most policies are written around the idea of a covered peril, which is a named event the policy says it will pay for. A sudden burst or discharge of water from a plumbing system is a common covered peril. Seepage over time, wear and tear, and lack of maintenance are commonly excluded.

Read the language in your own policy that begins with words like "sudden," "accidental," "continuous" or "repeated seepage." Those words often decide the outcome.

The word "often" appears in every row for a reason. Your policy decides, and two policies in the same neighborhood can read differently.

Flood and sewer backup are separate questions

Flood damage, meaning water that rises from the ground, a creek or a storm drain, is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies. Coverage comes from a separate flood policy, and FEMA notes that these typically carry a 30-day waiting period before they take effect, with exceptions.

Sewer and drain backup is another common gap. Many policies only cover it if an endorsement was added, and the limit can be lower than the damage. If your home has a finished basement, ask your agent whether you have that endorsement and how much it pays.

Our flood damage cleanup page explains how contaminated water is handled, and the sewage backup cleanup page explains the work on a Category 3 loss.

What the Pennsylvania Insurance Department says

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises homeowners to ask their insurer whether floods are covered, to photograph or record damage before cleanup and to contact the insurer as soon as possible. It also says to keep names, dates and receipts, to make only immediate repairs until the insurer approves permanent ones and to learn the total cost before agreeing to a settlement.

What to document before you call the insurer

Good records make a claim easier to explain and to evaluate. You do not need special tools, and a phone is enough.

  1. 1

    Photograph or record

    Take wide shots and close-ups of the damage before you move anything, and record the water line on walls.

  2. 2

    Note the source

    Write down what failed, where and when you found it, and keep the failed part if you can.

  3. 3

    Make safe, temporary repairs

    Shut off water and cover openings, and keep every receipt.

  4. 4

    Call your insurer

    Report the loss, record the name of the person you spoke to and the date.

A restoration crew can add moisture readings and a written scope, which helps when an adjuster compares what was wet with what was removed.

Mitigation is usually expected

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage once a loss happens. That is why extraction and drying matter even before an adjuster visits. Waiting days for a visit while water keeps soaking in can lead to more damage and a harder conversation about what the policy owes.

Our water extraction page covers pumping and removal, and the process for drying wet structure is on the structural drying page. Ask your insurer whether it has a required contractor process before you sign any work authorization.

What happens if the claim is denied

A denial is not always final. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department tells policyholders to ask for the reason in writing, to compare it with the policy language and to appeal or contact its Consumer Help Center if they disagree. A public adjuster or an attorney is another route some people take, though we do not give legal advice.

Questions about insurance and water damage in Abington

Will my homeowners policy pay for a burst pipe in Abington?

Sudden pipe failures are often covered, but coverage depends on your policy, your deductible and the cause. Ask your insurer and keep photos and receipts.

Is a slow leak covered?

Often not. Many policies exclude seepage and damage from long-term neglect. Read the wording about continuous or repeated leakage.

Do I need separate flood insurance in Abington?

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address and ask your agent about flood coverage.

Should I wait for the adjuster before drying my home?

No. Take photos first, then protect the property. Most policies expect you to prevent further damage, and delay can make it worse.
