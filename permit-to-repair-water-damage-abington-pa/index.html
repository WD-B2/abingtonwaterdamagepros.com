Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Abington, PA?

It depends on the repair and on the municipality. Here is who to ask in Abington Township, Jenkintown and Hatboro, and how to sort the work that likely needs one.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

24/7 emergency line

The short answer

Permit rules vary by town and by the kind of work, so ask the building department before you rebuild. In Abington Township, the Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road handles building, zoning, plumbing and HVAC permits. Jenkintown says permits are required before any work begins. Emergency drying and water removal are usually a separate step from permitted rebuilding.

This page does not list permit rules for specific repairs, because Abington's published page does not, and rules change. It explains how to ask the right questions.

Mitigation and rebuilding are different steps

Water damage work comes in two phases. Mitigation is the emergency stage that stops the source, removes water and dries the structure. Rebuilding is the repair afterward, such as replacing drywall, flooring and cabinets.

Mitigation often starts the same day, so permit questions usually belong to the rebuild. If the work involves plumbing, electrical, structural or HVAC changes, ask the building department before it starts.

Abington Township

The township's Community Development Department, at 1176 Old York Road, issues building, zoning, plumbing and HVAC permits. Electrical work is inspected by state-licensed inspectors. The page does not list rules for water damage repairs specifically, so ask what your repair needs.

What the local offices say

Abington Township lists the Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road for building, zoning, plumbing and HVAC permits, with electrical inspections by state-licensed inspectors. Jenkintown's borough page says permits are required before any work, that plumbing permits can be pulled only through master licensed plumbers and that residential permits are decided within 15 business days. The Borough Office is at 700 Summit Avenue, (215) 885-0700.

Jenkintown

Jenkintown's own site says permits are required before work begins. It also says plumbing permits go through master licensed plumbers, and that residential permits are decided within 15 business days. If your home is in the borough, call the Borough Office before demolition or rebuilding, and plan for that review time when you schedule contractors.

Our page on water damage restoration in Jenkintown explains how the borough's dense housing affects repairs.

Hatboro

Hatboro is a separate borough with its own permit process. We did not find rules for water damage repairs on a borough page, so contact the borough office and ask. Because Pennypack Creek runs through town, ask also whether floodplain rules apply to your address, and check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Our water damage restoration in Hatboro page covers the creek's role.

Pennsylvania contractor rules

Separately from permits, Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act applies to many repair jobs. Contractors must register with the Attorney General, and the registration number must appear in ads, contracts and estimates. Work over $500 needs a written, signed contract with a description, dates, the price and a cancellation notice. Verify a contractor at 1-888-520-6680.

How permits affect timing

A permit adds review time, so ask early. The drying phase does not wait, because wet materials get worse each day. Tell your contractor which parts need a permit and which do not, and ask them to sequence the work so drying continues while the permit is in review.

Our structural drying page explains why drying should not be delayed, and the burst pipe water damage page covers the plumbing repair that often needs a permit.

Questions to ask the building department

  1. 1

    What does my repair require?

    Describe the damage and the planned work and ask which permits apply.

  2. 2

    Who can pull the permit?

    Ask whether the owner or a licensed contractor must apply.

  3. 3

    How long is the review?

    Ask for the expected timeline and what inspections follow.

  4. 4

    Does floodplain apply?

    If you are near a creek, ask whether flood rules affect the work.

Why standards come up in permit conversations

Permit offices usually care about rebuilding, not drying. The drying itself is guided by ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, and documenting readings and steps helps both the permit file and an insurance claim.

How to ask the right office

When you call, describe the work in plain terms. Say whether walls are being opened, whether plumbing or wiring will be changed and whether the structure itself is affected. The answer often depends on those details.

Ask what documents the office wants, how long review takes and whether an inspection is needed before walls are closed. Write down the name of the person you spoke with and the date. If the office says no permit is needed, keep that note with your repair records.

Questions about permits in Abington

Do I need a permit to dry out my house?

Emergency drying is usually treated separately from rebuilding, but ask the building department. Permit questions mostly apply to the repair.

Who handles permits in Abington Township?

The Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road handles building, zoning, plumbing and HVAC permits.

Does a plumbing repair need a permit?

Plumbing work often does. In Jenkintown, plumbing permits go through master licensed plumbers. Ask your municipality.

Can the contractor handle the permit?

Often, but ask who is responsible in writing, and confirm the permit is issued before work starts.
