Water in a business is a downtime emergency

For a business, water damage is an emergency twice over. The building is getting damaged, and every hour you cannot open costs revenue. Our dispatch line runs 24/7, including nights and weekends when many commercial losses are discovered by an alarm or a neighboring tenant.

Start by stopping the water at its source if you can reach the valve, and clear people from areas with live electrical equipment. Move inventory, files and electronics off wet floors, and take photos of the damage and the storage areas before cleanup.

Call your property manager or landlord as well, because a supply line or roof may belong to the building and not the tenant.

What commercial water damage looks like

Commercial buildings differ from houses in ways that change how crews work. Space is larger, ceilings are often suspended tile, floors may be sealed concrete or commercial carpet, and shared walls and floors mean water in one suite can reach another.

Retail and shops

Inventory, shelving and display cases need quick protection and the floor needs fast extraction.

Offices

Ceiling tiles, carpet tile, desks and electronics are the main issues, and servers need special handling.

Restaurants and food service

Kitchen floors, walk-ins and drains matter, and health rules may require cleaning before reopening.

Medical and professional suites

Records, equipment and flooring may need documentation and controlled access.

Multi-unit and mixed-use

Water travels between units, and responsibility has to be sorted between owners and tenants.

How a commercial job is organized

Crews prioritize reopening. They extract water, protect valuables, set drying equipment and work in zones so part of a building can reopen while another part dries.

  1. 1

    Secure and assess

    The source is stopped, hazards are marked and the scope is walked with the manager.

  2. 2

    Extract and protect

    Water comes out, and contents are moved, covered or packed out.

  3. 3

    Dry in zones

    Equipment is placed by area, and moisture is mapped and logged.

  4. 4

    Document

    Photos, readings and a written scope are prepared for the insurer and landlord.

  5. 5

    Repair

    Ceilings, walls and flooring are restored, with scheduling around operations.

The documentation matters. Insurers and landlords ask for the cause, the affected areas, the materials removed and the drying logs, and a clear file makes a claim easier to explain.

Tenant, landlord and insurance questions

Responsibility for the repair depends on the lease and on where the water came from. A burst pipe inside a suite may be the tenant's issue under some leases, while roof and shared plumbing are often the landlord's. Read your lease and notify the landlord in writing.

Business property and business interruption coverage are separate from a building policy, and your policy language controls what is covered. We do not promise coverage or approval, and the pages on water extraction and structural drying explain the work that goes into the claim file.

What affects the cost

Commercial scope depends on floor area, number of rooms or suites, flooring type, ceiling system, contents and how many days of drying the building needs. After-hours work and access limits can also change the plan.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the best way to a real number for your building. Describe the type of space and the source and we will explain what a visit would assess.

Safety steps for a business

Clear people from affected areas, keep customers and staff away from wet floors and shut off power to wet equipment where it is safe to do so.

  • Post wet-floor warnings and block off the area.
  • Photograph damage and inventory before moving stock.
  • Note the time the water was found and who reported it.
  • Tell dispatch if the building is shared, because tenants and a landlord may need to be contacted.

Commercial drying plans follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

Questions about commercial water damage

Do you handle commercial water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes. Many commercial leaks are found after closing, so the line is staffed around the clock. Tell dispatch the building type and the source, and ask what to move before the crew gets there.

Can part of the building stay open while you dry the rest?

Often. Crews work in zones, and the plan depends on where the water went and what is safe to occupy.

Do you document the damage for the insurer and landlord?

Yes. Photos, moisture readings and the scope are written up so an adjuster and landlord can follow them.

Who pays, the tenant or the landlord?

It depends on your lease and the source of the water. Read the lease and notify the landlord in writing.
