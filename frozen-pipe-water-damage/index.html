Thawing pipes can turn into an emergency fast

A frozen pipe becomes an emergency the moment the ice melts, because a crack that was sealed by ice starts to leak. Our dispatch line runs 24/7, so call as soon as you see water, hear running water in a wall or find a pipe that is split.

Shut off the main water valve before anything else. If you cannot find a leak but a faucet runs slowly or not at all, leave the main closed until a plumber has looked, and keep the heat on in the house.

Never use an open flame, torch or kerosene heater to thaw a line. A hair dryer, a heat lamp or warm towels on an exposed pipe are the safer choices, and work from the faucet end toward the frozen section.

How ice splits a pipe

Water expands when it freezes, but the pipe rarely bursts where the ice sits. Ice forms a plug, and pressure builds in the trapped water between that plug and a closed faucet. The weak point gives way in that stretch of pipe, often a joint or a thin spot in the wall.

The split may stay hidden until the ice melts and pressurized water flows through it. That is why many frozen pipe losses are found hours after a cold night, when the house warms up or someone runs a tap.

This page describes the mechanism. How often it happens in a particular winter depends on the weather, and the National Weather Service publishes local freeze data for anyone who wants figures.

Where pipes are most exposed

Exterior walls

Supply lines in outside walls sit close to the cold and may have little insulation.

Unheated spaces

Garages, crawl spaces, attics and unfinished basement corners lose heat quickly.

Outdoor hose bibs

A hose left connected can freeze water inside the spigot and the line behind it.

Cabinets on cold walls

Sink supply lines in cabinets against exterior walls miss the room's warm air.

Vacant or low-heat rooms

A closed-off room or a home left cold while no one is there cools far below the rest of the house.

Prevention helps. Insulate exposed pipes, keep cabinet doors open on cold nights so room air reaches the lines, let a faucet drip during a hard freeze and disconnect hoses in the fall.

What a crew does after a frozen pipe splits

Frozen pipe damage is handled as a burst pipe with one extra step, which is checking the whole length of the line for more splits before the water is turned back on. A plumber repairs the pipe, and the restoration crew works on the building.

  1. 1

    Stop the flow

    The main is shut and the damaged section isolated so the structure stops getting wetter.

  2. 2

    Remove the water

    Pumps and wet vacuums clear floors, and wet insulation and soaked pad come out.

  3. 3

    Map moisture

    Meters and thermal imaging show water inside walls, ceilings and under floors.

  4. 4

    Dry the building

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the dry standard.

  5. 5

    Rebuild

    Drywall, insulation, flooring and paint are replaced after drying is verified.

If the line ran inside a wall near a sink or an outside wall, the wall and drywall repair page explains what the rebuild includes. A split in an upstairs line may also reach a ceiling, which our burst pipe page treats in more depth because the damage pattern matches.

What drives the cost of frozen pipe cleanup

The price depends on how long water ran after the thaw, how many rooms it reached and what the walls and floors are made of. Insulation in an exterior wall holds water, so those cavities often need to be opened.

Drying days and rebuild scope make up the rest. We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call gives you a real number for your own situation.

Sudden pipe failures are often covered by homeowners policies, while damage from neglect, such as leaving a home unheated, may not be. Read your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph everything before cleanup.

Frozen pipes in rentals and second-floor units

Renters and tenants in multi-unit buildings often find the loss after the pipe has already leaked into the unit below. If that is your situation, report the damage in writing to the landlord and call us for the drying side. Our water damage restoration in Jenkintown page covers the borough's dense buildings, where water in one unit commonly reaches another.

What to do when a pipe has frozen

If a pipe is frozen but not yet split, open the nearest faucet and keep the space warm. If it has split, shut off the main water valve at once.

  • Do not use an open flame on a frozen pipe.
  • Turn off power to wet areas if water is near outlets.
  • Photograph the split pipe and the damage before repairs.
  • Leave a trickle running only if a plumber tells you to.

After the pipe is repaired, the drying plan follows ANSI/IICRC S500.

Questions about frozen pipe damage

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Shut off the main water valve, then call and describe where the water is coming from. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Can I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

Often, if the pipe has not split. Use a hair dryer or warm towels, never an open flame, and keep the faucet open so melting water can flow. If you see any crack or leak, shut off the main and call.

Why did my pipe leak only after the weather warmed up?

Ice can plug a split, and the leak shows only when the ice melts. Check pipes after a cold snap even if nothing looked wrong.

Is damage from a frozen pipe covered by insurance?

Sudden pipe failures are often covered, while damage from leaving a home unheated may not be. Check your policy or ask your insurer.
