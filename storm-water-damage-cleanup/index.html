Emergency Storm Water Damage in Abington, PA

Storm water inside the house is an emergency

Storm water damage becomes an emergency as soon as rain has found a way in, because wet insulation and drywall can start to fail within a day or two. Our dispatch line runs 24/7, so call when the storm has passed and you can move safely. If the roof or a window is open, the first job is a temporary cover to stop more rain.

Stay away from downed power lines and call the utility or 911 about them. Do not go onto a wet or damaged roof, and keep people out of rooms with sagging ceilings.

Move belongings to dry areas, photograph the damage and openings, and place buckets or towels. A crew handles the water inside while a roofer or contractor handles the opening.

What counts as storm water damage

This page covers water that enters through the building envelope, which is the roof, siding, windows, doors and chimney. Rain driven sideways by wind can get through gaps that stay dry in a normal shower.

What the cleanup involves

  1. 1

    Make it safe

    The opening is covered, power hazards are handled and wet areas are isolated.

  2. 2

    Extract water

    Pumps and wet vacuums clear floors, and soaked insulation and pad are removed.

  3. 3

    Map moisture

    Meters and thermal imaging find water behind siding, above ceilings and in walls.

  4. 4

    Dry the structure

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference materials.

  5. 5

    Repair and restore

    Drywall, flooring and finishes are restored once the opening is repaired.

Roof and siding repairs belong to a roofer or exterior contractor. Plan on both trades, and ask the Abington Township Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road whether your repair needs a permit.

What affects the cost

Storm damage is priced by the number of rooms affected, how long the rain came in, the materials involved, the drying days and the rebuild scope. A single wet window wall costs less than a roof opening that let rain into the attic and two floors.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to a real number. Wind and rain damage is often covered by homeowners policies, while flood damage usually is not. Check your policy, and call your insurer promptly.

Storm season and preparation

Storms change how the year runs, and gutters, flashing and sump pumps are the usual weak points. Clear gutters, check the roof after high winds and test the sump pump before heavy rain.

After a storm, walk the attic with a flashlight for dark stains, wet insulation or daylight, which is the earliest sign of an opening.

Safety steps after a storm

Stay away from downed lines and anything wet near them. Do not enter a room with a sagging ceiling or standing water if the power is on.

  • Put a tarp or bucket under an active roof leak if you can do so safely from inside.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup for your insurer.
  • Do not use a generator indoors or in a garage.
  • Keep receipts for emergency repairs.

Storm water that comes from the ground or a drain is classified differently under ANSI/IICRC S500 than a clean rain leak.

Questions about storm water damage

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes. Storms often hit after dark, and the line is staffed around the clock. After the weather passes, call and say where rain got in.

Do I need a roofer or a restoration company?

Both, in most cases. A roofer fixes the opening, and a restoration crew dries and repairs the inside.

Is storm damage the same as flood damage?

No. Storm damage is rain that enters through the roof or walls, while flood damage is rising water from the ground or a creek.

Does homeowners insurance cover storm damage?

Wind and rain damage is often covered, while flooding usually is not. Check your policy.
