Water Damage Restoration in Glenside, Abington, PA

Glenside is split between Abington and Cheltenham townships, and this page covers the Abington side, including the Keswick area. Tookany Creek and a lot of paved ground shape how water behaves here.

Water damage on the Abington side of Glenside

Glenside is a census-designated place that straddles two townships. Part is in Abington Township and part is in Cheltenham Township, and ZIP code 19038 covers the area. This page is about the Abington side, and when you call, tell dispatch your street so the address can be placed correctly.

Keswick Village is the commercial center, with the Keswick Theatre, built in 1928 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1983. Homes surround it, and businesses sit among them, so crews see both houses and storefronts. Our commercial water damage restoration page covers business losses.

Tookany Creek and the Keswick drainage area

The township's planning documents say a floodplain runs along Tookany Creek through Glenside and skirts the Jenkintown boundary, with part preserved in Baederwood Park. The township's own stormwater page names the Keswick drainage area as one that has experienced excessive flooding, and says its recommended fixes for that area and one other could exceed $42 million.

Those are documented facts about specific drainage areas. They do not make every Glenside street flood-prone, and neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you check your own address.

Why paved ground matters

Abington's planning documents say nearly all of the township's part of the Tookany watershed has soils with low infiltration rates, which worsens runoff. In plain terms, when rain is heavy, water has few places to soak in and can collect in low spots, driveways and basement window wells.

If water enters a lower level, the first steps matter. Keep out while the power is on, do not run ordinary vacuums in standing water, and photograph the damage. Our flood damage cleanup page explains what a crew does with rising water.

Sewer backups

Heavy rain can also overload drains. When wastewater comes up through a floor drain, tub or toilet, it can carry contamination, and cleaning it is not a job for household fans and a mop.

Stay out of the area, keep children and pets away and call. Our sewage backup cleanup page explains how crews extract, remove and sanitize, and why porous materials that touched sewage are usually removed.

What a crew does, step by step

The steps below follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

  1. 1

    Assess

    Check moisture, find the source and identify the water category.

  2. 2

    Extract

    Pump or extract standing water from floors and low areas.

  3. 3

    Remove

    Take out materials that cannot be dried, such as soaked pad.

  4. 4

    Dry

    Run air movers and dehumidifiers and track moisture readings.

  5. 5

    Repair

    Restore drywall, flooring and finishes once the structure is dry.

Tenants and shop owners

Many Glenside buildings mix a shop below with apartments above, so water often crosses between occupants. If you rent, tell the landlord or property manager in writing the moment you find a leak, and tell dispatch whether the building has other tenants.

Owners of small businesses should photograph stock and equipment before anything is moved, and note which items were wet. That record helps the crew plan the work and helps an insurer understand what was affected.

Documentation and insurance

Take dated photos, keep receipts for emergency steps and contact your insurer promptly. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises recording names and dates of calls and learning the total cost before agreeing to a settlement.

Coverage depends on the cause of the loss. A burst pipe and rising creek water are treated very differently under most policies, so describe what happened accurately.

Questions from Glenside homeowners

Is Glenside in Abington Township?

Part of it is. Glenside also extends into Cheltenham Township, so confirm which township your address is in.

Does Keswick flood?

The township names the Keswick drainage area as one that has experienced excessive flooding. That is about a drainage area, so check your own address on the FEMA map.

Do you serve businesses in Keswick Village?

Yes. Crews handle commercial water losses as well as homes, and dispatch can explain what to expect.

What do I do if my drain backs up?

Stay out of the area, keep people and pets away and call. Sewage water is contaminated.
