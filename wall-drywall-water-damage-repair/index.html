What water does to a wall

A wall is a stack of layers. Paint covers drywall or plaster, which sits on studs with insulation between them, and water can enter any layer from above, below or inside. Drywall draws water upward from a wet floor, so a flood that is only an inch deep can stain the wall a foot or more up.

Callers report a bathroom wall with a soft spot, a kitchen wall damaged from siding, or a large hole where drywall gave way. Each means water has been in the wall long enough to weaken it.

If the wall is bowed or soft, keep people and furniture away, and do not push on it. Take photographs and find out whether water is still entering.

Signs the wall is wet

Stains and rings

Brown or yellow marks that spread, often with a darker edge.

Bubbling or peeling paint

Moisture pushes paint off the surface from behind.

Soft or crumbly drywall

The gypsum core loses strength and can break by hand.

Swollen baseboards

Trim and the bottom of the wall absorb water from the floor.

Musty smell

Odor with no visible cause may mean moisture inside the cavity.

A moisture meter and a thermal camera show how far the water went, which is often farther than the stain. Some walls are wet inside even when the surface looks dry.

How wet walls are handled

  1. 1

    Stop the source

    The leak or opening is repaired, and standing water is removed.

  2. 2

    Map the wall

    Meters and thermal imaging show wet areas and the edges of the damage.

  3. 3

    Open the cavity

    Baseboards come off, and small sections or holes are cut so air can reach the studs and insulation.

  4. 4

    Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference material.

  5. 5

    Replace and repair

    Wet insulation and damaged drywall are replaced and the wall is finished.

Cutting a wall open can feel drastic, but it is often the faster way to dry the cavity and check for growth. Where only the bottom of the wall is wet, crews usually cut a flood line two or four feet up and replace the lower section.

Drywall versus plaster

Many older homes have plaster on lath, which takes longer to dry and is harder to patch than drywall. Plaster can often be dried in place if it has not softened, while drywall that stays wet usually needs replacement.

Water that enters from outside

A wall can get wet from siding, windows or roof flashing. When that happens, the exterior must be fixed first, or the wall will wet again. The storm water damage page explains how wind-driven rain gets behind siding, and the ceiling water damage repair page covers water that comes from above.

If the damage came from a pipe inside the wall, the burst pipe page explains the sequence from shutoff to drying.

Finishing the repair

Finishing is where repair becomes visible. Seams are taped and mudded, texture matched and the wall primed and painted. Stain-blocking primer keeps old stains from bleeding through, and corners or ceilings may need repainting to blend the room.

Ask what is included in the written scope. Paint of the full wall, baseboard replacement and texture matching are the items most often left out of a rough estimate.

What affects the cost

Wall repair is priced by the wall area, whether insulation and baseboards come out, how many days of drying are needed and how much finishing is included. A single stained patch costs less than a wall that has to be opened along its length.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to a real number. Sudden leaks are often covered by homeowners insurance, while slow leaks may not be, so check your policy and ask your insurer.

Before anyone opens the wall

Turn off the circuit for any outlet in a wet wall, and do not touch switches with wet hands. Keep ceiling and wall cavities from being opened by anyone without protective gear if the water was not clean.

  • Do not paint over a stain, because it hides moisture.
  • Photograph stains, bulges and baseboards.
  • Remove items from the base of the wall.
  • Do not cut a wall open without a plan for drying the cavity.

Wall cavity drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500.

Questions about wall and drywall water damage

Can I just paint over a water stain on the wall?

Not until the source is fixed and the wall is dry. Paint over moisture traps it and the stain usually returns.

Do I have to replace all the drywall?

Not always. Crews cut out only the wet or weakened sections, often two or four feet up from the floor.

Is a bubbling wall dangerous?

It can mean the wall is holding water, and a bowed wall can fail. Keep clear and call for an assessment.

Does insurance cover wall water damage?

Sudden leaks are often covered, while slow seepage often is not. Check your policy.
