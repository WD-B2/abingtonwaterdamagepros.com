A failed appliance is an emergency until the water stops

An appliance leak turns into an emergency when the water gets under flooring or into a wall before anyone notices. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call once you have shut the machine off. A washer valve that breaks for even an hour can put water through a floor and into the room below.

Close the appliance's shutoff valve, which is usually a small handle or wheel behind the machine. If you cannot reach it, close the main water valve. Unplug the appliance only if the cord and outlet are dry, otherwise leave it and call the utility or 911.

Then lift rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor and photograph the scene for your insurer.

How each appliance usually fails

Appliances fail in patterned ways, and the failure tells a crew what kind of water to expect.

Gray water from a washing machine or dishwasher is treated more carefully than clean supply water because it can carry soap, food and bacteria. It still dries well when addressed quickly, but porous materials stay wet longer.

What the cleanup involves

Crews start with extraction and then work backward from the appliance, because water often runs under cabinets, behind trim and into the subfloor before anyone sees it.

  1. 1

    Extract

    Wet vacuums and extraction tools pull water from the floor and from under the appliance.

  2. 2

    Pull the appliance

    The machine is moved so the floor and wall behind it can be inspected and dried.

  3. 3

    Map moisture

    Meters and thermal imaging check flooring, baseboards and the wall behind.

  4. 4

    Dry and verify

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the dry standard.

  5. 5

    Repair

    Flooring, baseboards and drywall are repaired after drying is confirmed.

If the leak was in the kitchen, the kitchen water damage page covers cabinets and countertops, and the hardwood floor water damage page explains what happens when water gets under wood flooring.

What decides the price

Appliance leaks range from a quick extraction on a tile floor to a multi-floor loss where the water went through the ceiling below. Time matters most, so a leak caught within an hour or two is a smaller job than one found after a day.

Flooring type, how many rooms, water category and drying days set the scope. We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the best way to get a real number.

Insurers often treat sudden appliance failures differently from gradual leaks, so check your policy or ask your insurer before assuming. Keep the failed hose or part and photograph the model label.

Preventing the next leak

Replace rubber washer hoses with braided stainless hoses and check them every year or so. Turn off the washer's supply valves when you leave for a trip, and put a drain pan with a water sensor under machines on upper floors.

A leak sensor costs little and sends a phone alert, which can turn a floor-damaging flood into a mop-up. None of these steps removes the risk, but they shorten the time a leak runs unnoticed.

Safety steps before cleanup

Unplug the appliance only if the floor around it is dry, and shut the supply valve to it. If water has reached the outlet, leave it alone and turn off that circuit at the panel instead.

  • Do not stand in water near a plugged-in appliance.
  • Photograph the appliance label and the damage for your insurer.
  • Keep the failed hose or valve, because an adjuster may want to see it.

The restoration steps that follow are guided by ANSI/IICRC S500.

Questions about appliance leak water damage

Do you handle appliance leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes. A washer that floods on a Saturday night gets the same response process as a Tuesday morning call. Shut the valve, call the 24/7 line and describe how far the water spread.

Should I unplug the appliance first?

Only if the cord and outlet are dry and you can reach them safely. If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911.

Is dishwasher or washing machine water dangerous?

It can carry soap, food waste and bacteria, so avoid contact and wear gloves. Crews treat it as gray water and remove porous materials that soaked it up.

Will my homeowners policy cover an appliance leak?

Sudden failures are often covered, while slow leaks and wear often are not. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
