About Abington Water Damage Pros

We run a 24/7 water damage line for Abington Township, Jenkintown and Hatboro. This page explains how a call becomes a job and what we do and do not handle.

What Abington Water Damage Pros does

Abington Water Damage Pros takes emergency calls from people with water in their homes and businesses, and sends licensed and insured restoration professionals to deal with it. The work covers water extraction, structural drying, cleanup after pipe, appliance and storm losses, sewage backup cleanup and mold remediation after moisture.

The office is at 1495 Old York Road in Abington. It takes emergency requests by phone, so the office is not a walk-in counter, and the call line is the fastest way to start.

How a call becomes a job

The first call is a conversation, not a script. Dispatch asks what happened, where the water is and what it touched, and then tells you the first safe steps. Those steps depend on the source and might be shutting off the main, staying out of a room with electrical risk or photographing damage for an insurer.

Crews follow the written scope after assessment. The scope lists the rooms, the materials, the water category and the drying plan, and we explain the scope and the price before work starts.

Standards and how we talk about them

The industry standards for this work are published by ANSI and IICRC. S500 covers water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. We follow them as practice, and we suggest you ask any company you are comparing whether its crews work to them.

What we can say plainly is that the crews dispatched are licensed and insured professionals, and that you can ask for written details before work begins.

What we do not do

Honest limits matter more than a long list. We do not handle fire and smoke damage. We do not waterproof foundations or repair roofs, plumbing or siding, though a crew will tell you when one of those trades is the right next call. And we do not promise insurance outcomes or exact arrival times.

Insurance questions are answered in general terms on our pages, and your policy and your insurer have the final word.

What a scope contains

A scope is the written plan for a loss. It names each affected room and material, the category of the water and the equipment and steps planned to dry the structure. It also separates what must be removed from what can be saved, which is where most of the cost difference sits.

Water category matters because it changes the safety rules. Clean water from a supply line is handled differently from water that has touched a drain or a floor for days, and sewage needs the strictest protection. Moisture readings guide the drying plan, and the crew rechecks them until the structure reaches a dry reading.

What to do before the crew arrives

Before the crew arrives, you can help by shutting off the water at the main if it is safe to do so and staying out of rooms where water meets electrical outlets or appliances. Photograph the damage before anything is moved, because insurers usually ask for that record. Move valuables to a dry room if you can do it without risk.

None of this replaces the call. Dispatch can tell you which steps apply to your situation, and some, such as cutting power, depend on what the water has reached.
