A sagging or dripping ceiling is an emergency

A ceiling that sags, bulges or drips is holding more water than the drywall or plaster can bear, and it can fail without warning. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call as soon as you see it. While you wait, move people, pets and furniture away from the area underneath.

If water drips through a light fixture or near switches, leave the room and call the utility or 911 before us, and turn off the breaker only if you can reach it safely. A bulging ceiling can be relieved by poking a small hole at the lowest point over a bucket, but only if no electrical fixture is nearby and you stay out from under the sag.

Photograph the stain before anything moves. The size of the stain today and tomorrow helps both the crew and an adjuster.

What a ceiling stain tells you

The ring pattern of a stain is a clue, not a diagnosis. Water collects above the ceiling, spreads sideways along the joists and drips where the drywall is weakest, so the stain is often a few feet from the source.

Roof leaks that end up in the ceiling

A roof leak has no page of its own on this site because the damage lands in the same place, and the ceiling is where most homeowners first see it. Rain or snowmelt gets past shingles or flashing, soaks the decking and insulation, and then drips into the ceiling below.

Crews trace the path with a moisture meter and a thermal camera, which shows wet insulation as a cold patch. Drying the attic space matters as much as repairing the ceiling, because wet insulation holds moisture against the framing for weeks.

A roofer repairs the roof and a restoration crew handles the water inside, so plan on both trades. Storm-driven leaks are covered on our storm water damage page, which explains how wind-driven rain gets past the building envelope.

Repairing the ceiling after drying

Repair starts only when the space above and the drywall itself have dried to the standard. Mitigation and repair are separate stages, and rushing the second leaves wet framing behind new paint.

  1. 1

    Dry first

    Insulation and framing above are dried or replaced, and the ceiling is verified dry.

  2. 2

    Cut out the damage

    Sagging, stained or crumbling drywall or plaster is cut back to solid material.

  3. 3

    Patch or replace

    Small areas are patched, while larger sections or whole sheets are replaced.

  4. 4

    Prime and finish

    A stain-blocking primer, texture and paint match the room.

Plaster ceilings, common in older homes, are more fragile and take more care to patch than drywall. Ask for the scope in writing so you can see what is being replaced and what is being repaired.

What the estimate depends on

Ceiling repair is priced by size, height, access and what is hidden above. A single stained panel costs less than a sagging section that took insulation with it, and a vaulted ceiling needs scaffolding.

Water category, drying time and the finish you want also move the number. We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to get one for your ceiling.

Sudden leaks from a roof or a failed supply line are often covered by homeowners insurance, while slow leaks and maintenance issues often are not. Check your policy and keep photos of the stain as it grows.

Questions about ceiling water damage

Do you handle ceiling water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

We do. The line takes calls around the clock, and a ceiling that has started to sag is exactly the kind of call that should not wait for morning. Tell dispatch where the water is dripping and what is above that spot.

Should I poke a hole in a bulging ceiling?

Only to relieve a bulge that is about to fail, and only if no wiring or fixture is nearby. Stand clear, use a bucket and call for help right away.

Does a roof leak need a roofer or a restoration company?

Both. A roofer fixes the roof, and a restoration crew dries the wet insulation and framing and repairs the ceiling.

Can a stained ceiling just be painted over?

Not until the source is fixed and the area is dry. Paint over a wet or active leak will show the stain again, and the wet material can grow mold.
