Carpet Water Damage in Abington, PA

Carpet can often be saved if clean water is removed quickly, but the pad under it rarely survives. Call the 24/7 line to learn whether yours can be dried or must go.

Can wet carpet be saved?

Carpet is made of a surface fiber, a backing and a pad underneath, and each reacts to water differently. Clean water caught within about a day often leaves the carpet itself dry-able, while the pad usually holds water and may need to be replaced.

Contaminated water changes the answer. Carpet soaked with sewage or flood water is usually discarded, and carpet that sat wet for days can grow mold and smell.

If you pulled up a corner and found dampness or dark growth, stop and call before you pull more.

Carpet tile, wool and berber each have their own quirks, so a crew checks the label and the backing before deciding.

How crews work on wet carpet

Extraction starts with a weighted tool that presses the carpet and draws out water. Then the crew lifts a corner, checks the pad and tack strip, and decides whether to float the carpet over air movers or remove the pad.

  1. 1

    Extract water

    Weighted extraction tools remove water from carpet and pad.

  2. 2

    Check the pad

    Wet pad is cut out and discarded, and the subfloor is checked.

  3. 3

    Float and dry

    Carpet is lifted and supported on air movers so air passes under it.

  4. 4

    Treat

    Antimicrobial treatment is applied to carpet and subfloor if the water was gray or the carpet sat wet.

  5. 5

    Reinstall or replace

    Dried carpet is restretched, or replaced if it cannot be saved.

Replacing pad is usually cheaper than treating a mold problem later. The subfloor below, which our structural drying page covers, needs to dry before new pad goes down.

Water sources that hit carpet most

Washing machines, upstairs bathrooms, water heaters and basement seepage all wet carpet in different ways. A water heater failure is covered on the water heater leak cleanup page, and wet finished lower levels are covered on the basement water damage page.

Where water has soaked baseboards and the wall bottoms, the wall and drywall repair page explains how far up the damage travels.

What affects the estimate

Carpet cost depends on how many square feet are wet, whether the water was clean, how long it sat, whether the pad can be replaced in place and whether the subfloor needs drying. New carpet or pad adds to the repair scope.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to a real number. Insurance often covers sudden water damage to carpet, while long-term seepage may not be covered. Check your policy, ask your insurer and keep photos of the wet area.

Carpet moisture and mold

Carpet and pad that stay damp can grow mold, and mold may appear on the underside before it shows on top. If you see dark spots or smell musty odors, read the mold remediation page and call for an assessment.

The standard behind carpet decisions

Whether carpet and pad are kept or replaced follows the water category and how long they stayed wet, as described in ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Clean water caught early is the best case, and contaminated water usually ends the question.

Questions about carpet water damage

How long can carpet stay wet before it must be replaced?

Clean water caught within about a day often allows drying in place. After several days, or with gray or contaminated water, replacement becomes more likely.

Do I have to replace the pad?

Usually yes. Pad absorbs water and dries slowly, and replacing it is much cheaper than dealing with mold later.

Can I dry carpet with a household fan?

Fans help with a small spill, but they do not dry the pad or subfloor. Larger wet areas need extraction and controlled drying.

Why does my carpet smell after it dried?

A lingering odor means moisture or growth may remain in the pad or subfloor. Ask a crew to check with a meter.
