When a damp floor or high water bill becomes a slab leak emergency

Callers rarely say slab leak first. They say the floor feels warm in one spot, the carpet is damp with no obvious source, the water meter keeps moving with every tap closed, or the water bill jumped. Each of those can mean a pressurized line is leaking under a concrete slab.

This bridge matters because the problem is hidden. A slab leak is an emergency in the sense that a pressurized line keeps running, so call our 24/7 line as soon as you suspect it and shut off the main water valve while you wait for a plumber.

If the main is off and the meter stops moving, the leak is somewhere in the house or under it. If the meter still moves, the leak is probably between the meter and the house.

Signs that point to a leak under the slab

Warm or hot spots

A leaking hot line warms the concrete above it, which you may feel with bare feet.

Sound of running water

A hiss or trickle when every fixture is off can come from a pressurized line beneath the floor.

Damp flooring or baseboards

Carpet, laminate and wood show moisture with no visible source above.

Cracks or lifted tile

Water under a slab can push on flooring and grout over time.

Rising water use

The meter spins with the house shut down, or the bill jumps without a reason.

None of these signs is proof, and a plumber has to confirm the leak with listening and thermal equipment. Once it is located, repair options include opening the slab to fix the spot, rerouting the line through walls or the attic, or replacing the line.

How restoration fits with the plumbing repair

The plumber fixes the line, and the restoration crew handles the building. Because the water came up through the slab, flooring and subfloor materials are usually wet from below, and the slab itself holds moisture for a long time.

What the slab leak damage looks like

Water moves sideways under the flooring before it surfaces, so the wet area is often larger than the damp spot you see. Wood and laminate lift and cup, while carpet and pad hold water that dries slowly and can smell.

Crews check walls too, since water wicks up the bottom of drywall from the floor. Our wall and drywall water damage repair page explains how far up and how far across the damage can reach, and the hardwood floor page explains the flooring side.

What shapes the estimate

Slab leak cleanup varies with how long the line leaked, how much flooring is wet, whether the water was hot, how many days of drying are needed and how much of the floor needs rebuilding. The plumbing repair is priced by the plumber, separately.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to get a real number once you describe the symptoms.

Insurance often distinguishes a sudden break from a long hidden leak, and some policies exclude damage from a slow leak. Read your policy and ask your insurer, and keep the plumber's report.

Homes without slabs

If your home sits over a basement or crawl space, a hidden leak behaves differently. The basement water damage and crawl space water damage pages cover those, and the pages for Jenkintown and Hatboro cover homes in those boroughs.

What to do before the crew arrives

Shut off the main water valve if you hear running water under the floor or see warm spots. Keep an eye on the water meter, which keeps moving when a line is leaking.

  • Do not break into the slab yourself.
  • Photograph damp floors, baseboards and the meter reading.
  • Move rugs and furniture off wet flooring.
  • Call a plumber for the leak itself, since a restoration crew dries the damage but does not repair the pipe.

Drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500.

Questions about slab leak water damage

Do you handle slab leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes. The line answers 24/7, so if you find a warm, wet floor on a weekend, shut off the main and call. Dispatch will go through what to check and what a restoration visit would cover.

How do I know whether the leak is under the slab?

Close every fixture and look at the water meter. If it still moves with the main open, a plumber can use listening and thermal tools to find the line.

Can the slab dry in place?

Often, but it takes time, and crews verify with moisture readings before new flooring goes down. Flooring laid too soon over a wet slab can fail.

Does homeowners insurance cover slab leaks?

Coverage depends on the policy and the cause. Sudden breaks are often covered more readily than slow leaks, so ask your insurer.
