Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill? Finding a Leak in Abington, PA

A high bill with no change in how you live is a clue. This guide shows a simple meter test and where hidden leaks usually hide.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

24/7 emergency line

(445) 263-2078Call now

The short answer

If your water bill jumps and nothing has changed in how you use water, check for a leak. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then look at your water meter. If the meter keeps moving, water is flowing somewhere. Check toilets first, then outdoor spigots, then look for damp spots, warm floors or the sound of running water, and call a plumber if you cannot find it.

This test is a general method. Your utility's own advice and meter layout can differ, so contact it if you need help reading the meter.

Why bills jump without warning

Leaks that run at low volume can go unnoticed for weeks. A toilet that runs, a supply line with a pinhole or a hidden line under a floor all use water that never reaches a faucet. The bill is the first clue, and sometimes the only one.

Other causes exist, such as a billing estimate, a change in household size or irrigation use. Check the bill for whether the reading was actual or estimated before you chase a leak.

A simple meter test

You need five minutes and nothing else.

  1. 1

    Stop all use

    Close faucets, stop the washer and dishwasher and make sure no one flushes.

  2. 2

    Find the meter

    It is usually near where the water line enters, outside near the curb, in a box, or in the basement.

  3. 3

    Read the dial

    Note the leak indicator, often a small triangle or dial that spins when water moves, or write down the number.

  4. 4

    Wait

    Wait 15 to 30 minutes without using water, then check again.

  5. 5

    Compare

    If the indicator moved or the number rose, water is flowing somewhere.

If the meter moved, close the main valve and check again. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving with the main closed, the leak may be on the utility side of the valve.

Hidden damage and what it costs

A leak that runs for weeks can soak insulation, drywall and floors before you see a stain. A higher water bill can be followed by a larger repair bill. If you find a leak, stop it first, then check surrounding materials for moisture with a meter or a thermal camera.

Our water extraction page explains what happens when a leak has left water on a floor, and the burst pipe water damage page covers a pressurized line that has failed.

Water service in Abington

Most Abington Township residents get public water from Aqua, and some properties use private wells, according to the township. If you are on public water, your bill and meter come from Aqua, and the utility can answer questions about meter readings and estimated bills. If you are on a well, there is no utility bill to jump, and a hidden leak may show up as a pump that runs more often.

When to call a plumber and when to call a restoration crew

Call a plumber when the meter test shows a leak and you cannot find it, when the leak is in a pipe, or when a fixture needs repair. Call a restoration crew if you find water damage, such as wet drywall, a stained ceiling or damp flooring.

If the leak has already caused damage, the two steps happen in parallel. The plumber stops the leak, and the crew dries what got wet.

Insurance and the bill itself

Ask your insurer whether any part of the extra water cost is covered, and ask your utility whether it has a leak adjustment policy. Damage from a sudden leak may be covered by your homeowners policy, while slow leaks often are not, so check your policy and your insurer.

Leaks in nearby boroughs

Nearby towns have their own utilities and rules. If your home is in Jenkintown or Hatboro, see our pages on Jenkintown and Hatboro.

Reading your bill for clues

Compare the usage on the new bill with the same period last year, and with the last few months. A steady higher number can mean a constant leak, while a spike can mean a one-time event such as a filled pool or a stuck valve. Check whether the bill is based on an actual meter reading or an estimate, because an estimate can correct itself later.

If the numbers look wrong and the meter test shows no leak, contact the water provider and ask them to review the reading. Keep a record of the date and the person you spoke with.

Questions about water bill jumps in Abington

How do I know if my water bill is estimated?

The bill usually says whether the reading was actual or estimated. Ask the utility if you cannot tell.

Can a toilet raise my water bill that much?

A toilet that runs continuously can use a large amount of water over days, so check the tank first.

Is a leak always visible?

No. Leaks inside walls, under floors and underground can go unseen until damage or a high bill shows up.

Should I shut off the water while I look?

Yes, closing the main valve stops the flow while you check the meter and fixtures.
