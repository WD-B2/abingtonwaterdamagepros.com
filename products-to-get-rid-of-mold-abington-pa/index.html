Products to Get Rid of Mold in Abington, PA: What Works

A spray bottle can clean mold off a hard surface, but it cannot fix the moisture that made it grow. This guide sorts what products can do from what they cannot.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

24/7 emergency line

(445) 263-2078Call now

The short answer

For a small patch of mold on a hard, non-porous surface, the EPA says to scrub it with detergent and water and dry the area completely. No product replaces fixing the leak or humidity behind it, because mold grows where moisture stays.

If mold covers more than about 10 square feet, a patch roughly 3 feet by 3 feet, or follows a lot of water damage or contaminated water, the EPA says to consider hiring a professional. That includes growth inside drywall, carpet or insulation.

What "getting rid of mold" really means

Mold spores are in most indoor air, and they grow only when moisture is present. Removing visible growth without removing the moisture invites it to return, which is why many treatments fail. A product that kills growth on a surface does nothing for the leak under the sink.

That is also why this page is about products and not promises. No treatment is permanent, because new moisture can restart mold.

Read the label on every product and never mix cleaners. Ventilate the room, wear gloves and eye protection, and stop if you feel unwell.

Porous versus non-porous surfaces

The surface matters more than the product. Non-porous surfaces such as tile, glass, sealed metal and hard plastic can often be cleaned. Porous materials such as drywall, carpet, ceiling tiles and insulation hold growth inside them, and surface cleaning does not reach it.

Tile and glass

Scrub with detergent and water, and dry fully.

Sealed wood

Clean the surface and dry, and check the wood underneath.

Drywall

Growth that goes through the paper usually means cutting out the section.

Carpet and pad

Often discarded when growth is present, and the subfloor is checked.

If growth is on drywall or carpet, a crew explains the removal and containment steps on our mold remediation page, which follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.

Drying is the real product

The most important step is drying the area. Fix the leak, then dry the materials with fans, dehumidification and airflow until readings match unaffected areas. A damp closet or a cabinet under a leaking sink may need more than a towel and a day of airflow.

Our structural drying page explains how moisture meters and air movers tell a crew when a space is dry. For a closet wet by a condensate line, see our AC leak water damage page.

What the EPA says about when to bring in a professional

The EPA's mold cleanup guidance says people can usually handle a moldy area under about 10 square feet themselves. It advises considering a professional if there has been a lot of water damage, if mold covers more than 10 square feet or if the damage came from sewage or other contaminated water. It also says to fix plumbing leaks and other water problems as soon as possible and to dry items completely.

Do not paint over it

Paint and sealers do not solve a moisture problem. A coating over damp, moldy material can trap moisture and peel, and the growth continues under it. If you want to repaint, fix the moisture, clean or remove the affected material, make sure it is dry and use primer made for the surface.

When products are the wrong tool

Stop and call a professional if any of these apply.

  1. 1

    Large area

    The growth covers more than about 10 square feet.

  2. 2

    Drywall, carpet or insulation are soaked or soft.

  3. 3

    Contaminated water

    The water came from sewage or flooding.

  4. 4

    Persistent smell

    A musty odor remains after the surface is cleaned.

  5. 5

    Unknown source

    You cannot find where the moisture is coming from.

Health questions about mold exposure belong to a physician, and we do not diagnose or make health claims.

What a professional adds

A crew finds the moisture source with meters and thermal imaging, sets up containment so spores do not spread, removes materials that cannot be cleaned and dries the space. They can also point you to independent clearance testing if you want a third party to verify the work.

What not to mix or use

Never mix cleaning products, and in particular never combine bleach with ammonia or acids, because the fumes can be dangerous. Ventilate the area and wear gloves, eye protection and a respirator rated for the job if you are cleaning a small hard surface.

Porous materials such as drywall, ceiling tile and insulation are usually removed instead of cleaned, because mold grows into them. Spraying a product over soft materials can leave the problem inside the material and the moisture still present.

Questions about mold products in Abington

Is bleach the best way to kill mold?

Bleach can lighten stains on hard surfaces, but it does not reach inside porous materials and it is dangerous when mixed with other cleaners. The EPA's cleanup guidance names detergent and water for hard surfaces.

Can I treat mold in drywall with a spray?

Surface sprays do not remove growth that has gone through the paper. Drywall with growth usually needs to be cut out.

How do I stop mold from coming back?

Fix the moisture source and keep the area dry. Lowering humidity with ventilation or a dehumidifier helps.

When should I stop DIY and call someone?

When the area is larger than about 10 square feet, when materials are soaked or when the water was contaminated.
