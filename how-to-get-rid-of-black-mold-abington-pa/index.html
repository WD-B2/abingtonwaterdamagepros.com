How to Get Rid of Black Mold in Abington, PA Homes

Dark spots on a wall make people think of one thing, but color does not tell you what mold it is. What matters is how much, where and what is feeding it.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

The short answer

You cannot tell what kind of mold you have from its color, and cleanup steps do not depend on it. For a small patch under about 10 square feet on a hard surface, the EPA says to scrub with detergent and water and dry completely. For larger areas, soaked materials or contaminated water, it advises considering a professional. In every case, find and fix the moisture first.

Health questions about mold exposure belong to a physician. This page is about the building, not about diagnosing anyone.

Why "black mold" is a confusing term

Many molds look dark. Dark green, gray, brown and black patches show up in bathrooms and basements, and they can look similar to soot, dirt or old stains. A lab test can identify species, but the cleanup approach does not change based on which one it is.

What drives the work is the amount of growth, the materials it is on and whether the area is still wet. A small patch on tile is a different job from dark growth that has gone through the paper on a ceiling.

Why it grows where it does

Mold needs moisture and a surface to feed on. Dark growth often shows up in places that stay damp or cool and get little airflow.

What you can do about a small patch

If the area is small and on a hard surface, you can clean it safely by following a few steps.

  1. 1

    Fix the moisture

    Repair the leak or reduce humidity, because cleaning alone will not hold.

  2. 2

    Protect yourself

    Wear gloves and eye protection and ventilate the room.

  3. 3

    Scrub the surface

    Use detergent and water on hard surfaces, as the EPA suggests.

  4. 4

    Dry completely

    Use fans or a dehumidifier until the area is fully dry.

  5. 5

    Watch the area

    Check it again over the next weeks for returning growth or dampness.

Do not mix cleaning products. Bleach mixed with ammonia or acids produces dangerous gas, so use one product at a time and follow the label.

When it is not a small patch

Some situations need more than a scrub brush. If growth is on drywall, carpet, ceiling tile or insulation, it has likely gone below the surface. If the area is bigger than about 10 square feet, if materials are wet or soft or if the water came from sewage or flooding, stop.

Our mold remediation page explains containment, removal and verification, and why a crew follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard. A leak behind the wall is the other trap, so the wall and drywall water damage repair page explains how wet cavities get dried.

Getting the moisture out

Moisture control is the long-term answer. Run exhaust fans in bathrooms, fix drips quickly and keep indoor humidity lower with ventilation or a dehumidifier. Do not store boxes tight against cold exterior walls, and keep airflow around furniture.

If the area is still damp, drying equipment may be needed. Our structural drying page explains how professionals measure moisture and know when materials are dry.

What the EPA says

The EPA's mold cleanup guidance says that for a moldy area under about 10 square feet, most people can handle cleanup by following its tips, and that hard surfaces can be scrubbed with detergent and water and dried completely.

It advises considering a professional when there has been a lot of water damage, when mold covers more than 10 square feet or when the damage came from contaminated water, and it says to fix plumbing leaks and other water problems as soon as possible.

What not to do

Do not paint over it

Coatings trap moisture and the growth continues underneath.

Do not dry-scrub

Brushing dry growth can spread spores through the room.

Do not ignore the source

A leak or a damp wall will feed new growth.

Do not mix chemicals

Mixed cleaners can release dangerous gases.

Leaks that start the problem

The leak is often upstairs or behind a wall. A bathroom ceiling stain can come from the floor above, and a closet patch can come from an air conditioner line. See our ceiling water damage repair page for stains that spread, and the kitchen water damage page for under-sink leaks.

Why the color does not tell you much

People search for black mold because it sounds like the worst case, but color alone does not identify a mold or say how risky it is. Mold comes in many colors, and the cleanup steps for most are the same. What matters is how large the area is, what it is growing on and whether moisture is still feeding it.

That is why the first question is always where the water came from. A patch above a bathroom fan points to humidity, while a patch on a basement wall may point to seepage. Fix that source first, or the mold returns after cleanup.

Questions about black mold in Abington

Is black mold more dangerous than other mold?

Color does not identify mold type, and cleanup steps are driven by the amount and the materials. For health questions, ask a physician.

Can I get rid of mold with vinegar or bleach?

Detergent and water on hard surfaces is the EPA-named method. Neither product fixes moisture, and bleach is hazardous when mixed with other cleaners.

How do I know whether the mold is inside the wall?

A moisture meter and thermal imaging show wet areas inside walls, and a stained, soft or bubbling wall is a clue.

How big is too big for a DIY cleanup?

The EPA's rule of thumb is about 10 square feet, roughly a 3 foot by 3 foot patch.
