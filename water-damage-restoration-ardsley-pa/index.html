Water damage in Ardsley homes

Ardsley is an unincorporated community in Abington Township, partly extending into Upper Dublin Township, with ZIP code 19038. Ardsley Park, the Ardsley Wildlife Sanctuary and a station on SEPTA's Warminster Line are its best-known landmarks.

Water damage in a house can come from a pipe, an appliance, a roof or the ground, and no community is exempt. This page explains the first steps and how restoration works, and it adds what the township itself has said about drainage in Ardsley.

What the township has done about drainage

Abington Township's project list says the Edge Hill Road and Tyson Avenue reconstruction, completed in fall 2021, aimed to alleviate existing flooding and erosion problems. The work included drainage along cross streets and the reconstruction of Ardsley Avenue, Edgecomb Road and Custer Avenue.

The same list describes routing stormwater through the Ardsley Wildlife Sanctuary, with an enlarged basin, rock-lined pools and a level spreader to slow flows. The township's planning documents say it has built detention basins, including four in the sanctuary. These are township infrastructure projects. They show that stormwater was a concern near Edge Hill Road, but they do not tell you whether your house will ever take on water.

Check your own address

FEMA flood-zone status for individual Ardsley streets is not verified on this page. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows the mapped zone for an address, and Zone X does not mean flooding cannot happen. Most Pennsylvania homeowners policies exclude flood, so ask your agent what yours covers.

Water backup coverage is a separate question. Some policies exclude water that comes up through drains unless an endorsement adds it, so it is worth reading before storm season.

A leak at home, step by step

Shut the water off where the line enters the house, or at the valve for the fixture. Keep clear of wet outlets, and do not stand in water to touch a panel. If a ceiling is bulging, stay out of the room.

Photograph the damage, lift valuables off wet floors and call. Dispatch will ask where the water started, how deep it is and what it touched. Our pages on burst pipe water damage, ceiling water damage repair and wall and drywall water damage explain how crews handle the most common sources.

Drying a house properly

Wet is not dry until the readings say so. Surfaces can feel dry while framing and subfloor stay wet, and trapped moisture is how mold gets a start. Crews use moisture meters and sometimes thermal imaging to find water that has traveled, and run air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are in the dry range, as the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes.

If a musty smell returns after a leak, tell the crew. The EPA says to fix leaks quickly and dry materials completely, and to call a professional for areas larger than about 10 square feet or when the water was contaminated.

Insurance basics

A claim is easier to describe with dated photos, a note of what you found and when, and receipts for emergency steps. Contact the insurer promptly and keep records of every call. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises learning the total cost before you settle.

Coverage depends on the cause and your policy. A restoration scope does not guarantee payment, and the insurer decides coverage.

Questions from Ardsley homeowners

Is Ardsley in Abington Township?

Yes, though part of the community also extends into Upper Dublin Township. Check which township your address is in.

Did the township fix flooding in Ardsley?

The township reports a reconstruction of Edge Hill Road and Tyson Avenue, completed in fall 2021, to address flooding and erosion, and basins in the Wildlife Sanctuary. That does not guarantee any property stays dry.

What should I do if my basement takes on water?

Stay out while the power is on, photograph the water line and call. Do not run ordinary vacuums in standing water.

Does homeowners insurance cover a backup through the drain?

Often only with an endorsement. Read your policy and ask your insurer before a loss.
