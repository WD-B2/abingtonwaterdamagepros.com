Emergency Basement Water Damage in Abington, PA

A flooded basement is a time-sensitive emergency

Water in a basement is an emergency because it sits against walls and floors that dry slowly and often hold the home's utilities. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call as soon as you see water. While you wait, stay out of the basement if the water is deep or touches the electrical panel.

If water is rising near the panel, outlets or the furnace, do not go down. Call the electric utility or 911. If you can safely cut power from above, do so, and never stand in water to touch a breaker.

Photograph the water line, the stored items and the source, from the stairs if needed.

Where basement water comes from

Sump pump failure

The pump fails or cannot keep up, and the pit overflows after heavy rain or a power outage.

Groundwater seepage

Saturated soil pushes water through floor cracks and wall joints.

Window wells and doors

Water pools outside and enters through wells, windows or bulkhead doors.

Plumbing leaks

A supply line, water heater or washer in the basement leaks onto the floor.

Floor drain backup

Water rises through a floor drain, which may mean a sewer problem.

The source decides the cleanup. A clean-water plumbing leak is straightforward, seepage and surface water may be contaminated, and a floor drain backup is handled under sewage rules on our sewage backup cleanup page.

Finished and unfinished basements

A bare concrete basement cleans up quickly, but finished space has carpet, drywall, insulation and furniture that soak up water. Crews pull wet carpet and pad, cut drywall above the water line and move contents to dry space.

  1. 1

    Pump and extract

    Submersible pumps and wet vacuums remove standing water.

  2. 2

    Remove wet materials

    Carpet, pad, wet insulation and soaked drywall are removed.

  3. 3

    Dry the walls and floor

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run, with moisture readings taken on concrete, framing and walls.

  4. 4

    Clean and treat

    Surfaces are cleaned, and antimicrobial treatment is applied if the water was contaminated.

  5. 5

    Restore

    Drywall, flooring and trim are replaced after drying.

Our carpet water damage and wall and drywall pages cover the materials that most often come out of a finished basement.

Restoration is not waterproofing

A cleanup crew dries what is wet. It does not seal foundation walls or stop groundwater from coming in, and callers who ask about waterproofing or foundation inspection deserve a straight answer. If water keeps returning, a waterproofing or drainage contractor, or a structural engineer for foundation cracks, is the right call.

We can dry the basement and document the damage, and your contractor can fix what lets the water in. Ask any contractor to explain the plan in writing.

Sump pumps and heavy rain

Test your sump pump before heavy rain, check that the discharge line runs away from the house and consider a battery backup if power outages are a concern. A pump that fails during a storm often overflows within hours.

Water that arrives from a creek or mapped flood area is covered on the flood damage cleanup page, and you can check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Our Meadowbrook page covers the Meadow Brook valley that the township describes as its largest floodplain.

What affects the cost

Basement cleanup depends on water depth and area, finished or unfinished space, the water category, materials to remove, drying days and how much is rebuilt. A shallow leak on concrete is far smaller than a flooded finished lower level.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to a real number. Standard homeowners policies often exclude groundwater and flood but may cover a sudden plumbing failure. Check your policy and ask your insurer about water backup or flood coverage.

What to do and what not to do

Stay out of a flooded basement while the power is on, because standing water can carry current from outlets and panels. Do not walk down stairs into water you cannot see through.

  • Do not run a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Turn off power at the main only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Photograph the waterline before anything is moved.
  • If the water is dark, smells like sewage or came through a drain, treat it as contaminated and wait for the crew.

Contaminated water is classified and handled under ANSI/IICRC S500, which is why the source matters.

Questions about basement water damage

Do you handle basement flooding emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes. Heavy rain rarely waits for business hours, so the line stays open all night. Call, tell dispatch the depth and whether the sump is running, and ask what is safe to move.

Is basement cleanup the same as waterproofing?

No. Cleanup removes water and dries the space, while waterproofing or drainage work stops water from entering. Ask a waterproofing contractor about the second.

Can I pump the basement out myself?

Small amounts, yes. Deep water pressing on walls or touching electrical equipment is a job for professionals, and pumping too fast can stress foundation walls.

Will insurance cover a flooded basement?

It depends on the source. Sudden plumbing failures are often covered, while groundwater and flood usually need separate coverage.
