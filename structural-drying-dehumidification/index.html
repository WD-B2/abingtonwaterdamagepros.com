Why does the house still feel damp after the water is gone?

A house can look dry and still be wet. Water soaks into drywall, framing, insulation and subfloors, and it moves out slowly as vapor. If a room smells musty, feels humid or has walls that are cool to the touch days after cleanup, the structure may still be holding water.

Structural drying is the process that removes that hidden moisture on purpose. It is also called water mitigation, which is the emergency stage that stops damage from spreading before repair begins.

Skipping it is the most common reason repairs fail. New paint, flooring or trim over damp materials traps moisture and invites staining, warping and mold.

How moisture is mapped

A crew starts by finding where the water went, using tools instead of guesses.

Moisture meters

Pin and pinless meters read the moisture content of wood, drywall and plaster.

Thermal imaging

A thermal camera shows cool, wet areas behind surfaces without cutting them open.

Hygrometers

Temperature and humidity readings show how much moisture the air holds.

Reference readings

Dry areas of the same material set the target the wet areas must reach.

The map is repeated on each visit, so progress is measured and not assumed. This is the basis of the ANSI/IICRC S500 approach, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to that standard.

Drying equipment and what it does

Equipment used in structural drying
EquipmentWhat it doesWhere it is used
Air moversMove air across wet surfaces to speed evaporationFloors, walls, ceilings, carpet
LGR dehumidifiersPull moisture out of the air so evaporation keeps goingMost residential drying jobs
Desiccant dehumidifiersDry the air in cold or very humid conditionsLarger or colder jobs
Injection and mat systemsMove air into wall cavities or under hardwoodWalls with insulation, hardwood floors
Air scrubbersFilter the air during and after demolitionJobs with debris or odor

Equipment placement depends on how the room is laid out and where the water traveled. Callers often ask about renting air movers and dehumidifiers. Drying equipment comes with a restoration job so the crew can place it and measure results, and a call can tell you what fits your situation.

How long drying takes

Drying time depends on the materials, how wet they got, the room temperature and humidity, and the equipment on site. Many residential jobs take a few days, and thick or saturated materials such as hardwood, plaster and subfloors can take longer.

A crew checks readings on each visit and adjusts equipment. The job ends when wet materials match the dry reference readings, not on a calendar date.

What the estimate covers

Structural drying is priced by the number of rooms, the amount of equipment, the days it runs and the monitoring visits. A single room with a localized leak is smaller than a floor where water traveled through several rooms.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to a real number. Insurance often covers drying after a sudden loss, and the moisture logs a crew keeps can help document the claim. Read your policy and ask your insurer.

When drying and removal work together

Drying works on materials that can be saved. Sagging ceilings, soaked carpet pad and sewage-contaminated drywall are removed first, which our water extraction page and sewage backup cleanup page explain, and drying handles what remains.

Floors take special equipment, as the hardwood floor water damage page explains, and walls that wick water get opened and dried as covered on the wall and drywall page.

What to do and what not to do during drying

Do not turn off drying equipment to save noise or power, because stopping early is how damp framing ends up growing mold. Keep doors and windows closed in the drying area, as the crew directs.

  • Do not move equipment once it is placed.
  • Keep children and pets away from cords and ducting.
  • Tell the crew if the power trips.
  • Expect daily moisture readings until the structure reaches a dry reading.

The drying approach is described in ANSI/IICRC S500, and readings are taken against a dry reference.

Questions about structural drying

How do I know my house is actually dry?

Moisture readings on the wet materials match readings from unaffected areas of the same material. A crew records them on each visit.

Can I use my own fans and a store-bought dehumidifier?

They help with small spills. For water that soaked walls or floors, controlled drying with measurement works better and is more likely to satisfy an insurer.

What is the difference between water mitigation and structural drying?

Mitigation is the whole emergency stage, including stopping the source and removing water. Structural drying is the part that removes moisture from the building materials.

Is drying noisy?

Equipment makes a steady fan sound and runs around the clock for a few days, so plan for that in occupied homes.
