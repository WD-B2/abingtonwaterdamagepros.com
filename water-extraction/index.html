When standing water is an emergency

Standing water is an emergency because every hour it sits, it wicks further into drywall, soaks the subfloor and swells wood. Our dispatch line runs 24/7, so call the moment you see it, then use the minutes before a crew reaches you to stop the source if you can do it safely.

Shut off the main water valve for a pipe or supply leak. Keep people and pets out of the water if it could be contaminated, and lift valuables, rugs and electronics onto dry surfaces.

Stay out of any room where water touches outlets, a breaker panel or plugged-in appliances. If you cannot reach the panel with dry feet, call the electric utility or 911 before you call us.

What water extraction actually is

Water extraction is the removal of liquid water from a building, and it comes before any drying. Dehumidifiers and air movers handle moisture that is in the air and in materials, but they cannot clear a flooded floor, so pumps and extraction units go first.

A crew sizes the equipment to the water. Deep water needs a submersible pump with a hose run to a drain or outdoors, and shallow water on hard floors is pulled up with a wet vacuum or a truck-mounted extraction unit. Wet carpet gets a weighted extraction tool that presses the pad and draws water out of it.

What happens once the water is out

Removing the visible water is only half of the job. Water travels under baseboards, behind cabinets and into insulation, so the next step is moisture mapping with a meter and a thermal camera. The map shows what is wet, what can be dried in place and what has to come out.

Drying then follows the principles in the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, and our structural drying page explains the equipment and the readings that tell a crew when a building is dry. Ask any company you are comparing whether its crews work to S500.

If the water came from a failed supply line, the burst pipe page covers the source side of the job, and a flooded basement has its own concerns with sump pumps and floor drains.

Do these steps while you wait for the crew

  1. 1

    Find the source

    Look for a burst pipe, overflowing fixture or failed appliance and shut off its stop valve or the main.

  2. 2

    Cut risky power

    Turn off breakers only if the panel is dry and reachable, and unplug items that are not already in the water.

  3. 3

    Move contents

    Lift furniture legs onto blocks, take rugs and electronics to a dry room, and photograph the damage first.

  4. 4

    Open the room

    Open windows if weather allows and keep wet-area doors clear so crews can bring in hoses and equipment.

What shapes the price of an extraction job

People often call for a quote first, and the honest answer is that extraction is priced by scope. We explain the scope and the price before work starts, in plain words.

Depth and area matter most, followed by water category, because gray water from a washing machine or contaminated water from a sewer line needs protective gear and removal of porous materials. Access also counts, since a basement with narrow stairs takes longer to pump than a ground floor with a door to the yard.

Contents and the drying that follows are priced separately from the extraction. If you want a real number for your own water, call and describe it. Insurance often covers sudden and accidental water but not gradual leaks, so check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep photos and receipts.

Questions about water extraction in Abington

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes. The dispatch line answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call, describe how deep the water is and whether the source is stopped, and dispatch will start from there. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Is it safe to walk through the water?

Not until you know what it contains and what it touches. Water that reaches outlets or the panel can carry current, and sewage or flood water carries contamination. Stay out and call for guidance.

How long does water extraction take?

Pulling water out of one room can take a few hours, while a flooded basement or a multi-room loss takes longer. Drying afterward takes additional days.

Can I rent air movers and dehumidifiers instead of calling a crew?

Drying equipment comes with a restoration job so the crew can place it correctly and read moisture levels. Call and describe what you have, and dispatch can tell you what fits your situation.
