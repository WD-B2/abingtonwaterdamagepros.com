Water Damage in a Rental: Tenants and Landlords in Abington, PA

When water hits a rental, the tenant, the landlord and two insurance policies are all involved. This guide sorts out who does what and in what order.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

24/7 emergency line

(445) 263-2078Call now

The short answer

Tenants should stop the water if they can do so safely, photograph the damage and tell the landlord or property manager in writing right away. Landlords generally handle repairs to the building, and renter's insurance generally covers a tenant's belongings and sometimes temporary housing, though both depend on the lease and the policy. Keep copies of every message and photo.

This is general information and not legal advice. The lease, Pennsylvania law and your insurer decide the details.

First steps for a tenant

The first hour matters. Start with safety, then records.

  1. 1

    Make it safe

    Stop the source if you can reach the valve, and stay out of areas with electrical risk.

  2. 2

    Photograph the damage

    Capture the water, the source and your belongings before you move them.

  3. 3

    Notify in writing

    Email or text the landlord or manager, and keep a copy with the time.

  4. 4

    Call the emergency number

    Many leases list a maintenance line for water emergencies, so use it.

  5. 5

    Protect belongings

    Move items to dry areas and keep receipts for anything you buy to deal with it.

A written notice creates a record of when the landlord learned about the damage. A phone call is useful in an emergency, but follow it with a message.

Who is responsible for what

Typical responsibilities after water damage in a rental
ItemUsually the landlordUsually the tenant
Repairs to walls, ceilings, floors and plumbingYesNo
Drying and cleanup of the unitOften arranged by the landlord or property managerMay be asked to give access
Tenant belongings and clothingNoCovered by renter's insurance if you have it
Temporary housing costsDepends on the leaseSometimes covered by renter's insurance
Damage caused by tenant negligenceMay be charged to the tenantPossible liability

The word "usually" matters. Leases differ, and who caused the leak can change who pays. Read your lease and ask your insurer.

Renter's insurance versus the landlord's policy

The landlord's insurance covers the building. It does not cover your clothing, furniture or electronics. Renter's insurance covers those items for covered causes and may pay for living costs if the unit is uninhabitable, depending on the policy.

If you do not have renter's insurance, your belongings are generally at your own risk, which is why many tenants buy it. Ask your insurer what water causes are covered and whether there is a limit on high-value items.

Landlord steps

Landlords and property managers can reduce the damage and the dispute by acting quickly.

Stop the source

Shut off the water or arrange a plumber, and secure the unit.

Start drying

Water that sits for days damages materials and can grow mold.

Document

Photograph, record readings and keep invoices for the insurer.

Communicate

Tell tenants what will happen, when access is needed and how long it may take.

Water often passes between units, so check the unit below and the one beside the source. Our ceiling water damage repair page explains what happens when water drops through a ceiling, and the commercial water damage page covers multi-unit buildings, which have similar coordination issues.

Mold in a rental

Mold often follows water that was not dried. Tenants who find dark spots after a leak should photograph them and report them in writing. Landlords should dry the area and arrange an assessment. The mold remediation page explains how containment and verification work.

For health concerns, tenants should ask a physician. We do not provide health advice.

Notes for Abington-area rentals

Local building and permit offices can help when repairs are large. Abington Township's Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road handles building, zoning, plumbing and HVAC permits, and Jenkintown's Borough Office at 700 Summit Avenue handles permits in the borough. Landlords should ask which repairs need permits before rebuilding.

Getting the unit dry

Drying is the landlord's best protection and the tenant's best chance of staying in the unit. A crew uses moisture meters and air movers to dry walls and floors, and the work may last several days. Equipment is noisy, and tenants may need to plan around it. See our structural drying page for how it works.

If you cannot stay in the unit

If the unit is not livable, ask the landlord in writing about alternatives, and ask your renter's insurer whether it covers temporary housing. Keep receipts for hotel stays and meals. The lease may address rent during repairs, so read it and ask a tenant advocate or attorney if there is a dispute.

Rentals in nearby boroughs

Multi-unit rentals are especially common in compact boroughs. See our pages on water damage restoration in Jenkintown and Hatboro for what is different there.

Standards that guide the cleanup

Whoever pays, the cleanup itself should follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Ask for the scope and the moisture readings in writing.

Keeping records as a tenant or landlord

Records protect both sides. Tenants should photograph the damage, keep copies of messages to the landlord and note the date and time of each call. Landlords should document the repair requests they received, the date work began and the invoices from contractors.

If belongings were damaged, a tenant should make a list with descriptions and approximate values, since renter's insurance claims usually ask for one. Both sides benefit from written updates, even brief ones, because they show a clear timeline if there is a later disagreement.

Questions about water damage in a rental in Abington

Who pays to dry a rental unit after a leak?

Usually the landlord arranges and pays for building repairs and drying, but the lease and the cause of the leak matter.

Does the landlord's insurance cover my belongings?

Generally no. Renter's insurance is the usual source for a tenant's belongings.

Should I call a restoration company myself?

Tell the landlord first, because the building belongs to them. In a true emergency you can start protective steps and record them.

What if my landlord does not respond?

Keep documenting in writing, take photos and consider contacting a tenant advocate or the local housing office.
