Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps in Abington, PA

Filing a claim after water damage is easier when you work in order. This checklist follows the Pennsylvania Insurance Department's guidance and adds what restoration crews see.

  • By the Abington Water Damage Pros team
  • Published November 12, 2026

(445) 263-2078Call now

The short answer

File a water damage claim by stopping the water, photographing everything before cleanup, contacting your insurer as soon as possible, making only immediate repairs and keeping every receipt. Then learn the full cost before accepting a settlement, and appeal or contact the Pennsylvania Insurance Department's Consumer Help Center if you disagree with the outcome.

The steps below expand on that list. They are general guidance, and your policy sets the actual terms. Nothing here promises that a claim will be approved or paid.

Step 1: Make the situation safe

Before any paperwork, stop the water and protect people. Shut off the main valve or the appliance supply, and keep out of rooms where water touches outlets or the panel. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911.

Safety comes before photos. A wet panel or a sagging ceiling is not worth a better picture.

Step 2: Photograph and record everything

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises policyholders to photograph or record damage before cleaning up. Do it from several distances, and include water lines on walls, wet flooring, stained ceilings and damaged contents.

Wide shots

Show each room so the layout and the extent of the damage are clear.

Close-ups

Capture stains, swollen trim, bubbling paint and failed parts.

Contents

List and photograph furniture, electronics and papers that were damaged.

The source

Photograph the broken pipe, hose or opening and keep the part.

Add a short note for each photo with the date and time. If you cannot photograph something safely, describe it in writing.

Step 3: Call your insurer

Call your insurer as soon as you can, and write down who you spoke to, the date and what they said. Ask what the claim process looks like, whether the policy has a deductible for this kind of loss and whether the insurer requires specific contractors.

Ask for the claim number and keep it with your records. If you rent, call your landlord and your renter's insurer, because the building policy and your contents policy are different.

Step 4: Make only immediate repairs

The Insurance Department says to make only immediate repairs and wait for insurer approval before permanent ones. Immediate repairs include shutting off water, covering a roof opening, removing standing water and starting drying. Rebuilding drywall or flooring can wait until the structure is dry and the claim is moving.

Our water extraction and structural drying pages describe the mitigation work that insurers usually expect to happen quickly.

Step 5: Keep receipts and logs

Keep every receipt for supplies, equipment, hotel stays and repairs, and a log of calls and emails. A restoration crew can provide a written scope, photos and moisture readings, which help an adjuster see what was wet and what was removed.

Step 6: Learn the full cost before you settle

The Insurance Department advises policyholders to learn the total cost of the damage before agreeing to a settlement. A first estimate may miss hidden damage behind walls or under floors, so ask for a written scope that covers drying, demolition and rebuilding.

If the first payment seems low, ask the insurer how it was calculated and whether it can be adjusted once the drying is complete.

Step 7: If the claim is denied or disputed

Ask for the reason in writing and compare it with the policy language. The Insurance Department says to appeal the decision or contact its Consumer Help Center if you disagree. Keep the records from steps 2 to 6, because they are your evidence.

Local contacts that help with repairs

In Abington Township, the Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road handles building, zoning, plumbing and HVAC permits, and electrical work is inspected by state-licensed inspectors. A repair that needs a permit can affect timing, so ask early. In Jenkintown, permits go through the Borough Office at 700 Summit Avenue.

Common mistakes to avoid

Waiting to call

Late notice can complicate a claim, and the damage grows while you wait.

Throwing things out

Keep damaged items or photograph them before disposal.

Signing too fast

Read any work authorization or settlement before you sign.

Skipping drying

Rebuilding over wet materials creates new problems and new costs.

The standard adjusters expect

Restoration records are often compared against ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. Moisture readings, equipment logs and dated photos make that comparison easy for an adjuster.

Questions about water damage claims in Abington

How soon should I report water damage to my insurer?

As soon as you can. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises contacting the insurer promptly and recording names and dates.

Can I start repairs before the adjuster visits?

Make only immediate repairs, such as stopping water and drying. Wait for insurer approval before permanent repairs, and keep receipts.

Does a restoration company file the claim for me?

The claim is yours to file, but a crew can document the damage and provide a written scope for your adjuster.

What if the insurer says the damage was gradual?

Ask for the reason in writing and compare it with your policy. You can appeal or contact the Consumer Help Center.
