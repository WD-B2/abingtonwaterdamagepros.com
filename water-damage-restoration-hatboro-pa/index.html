When water is spreading in Hatboro

Water spreading through a Hatboro home or shop is an emergency, and the 24/7 line takes the call at any hour. If the water is rising from the creek or a storm drain, stay out of it and away from electrical equipment. If a pipe has failed, close the main valve first.

Dispatch will ask where the water entered, how deep it is and whether utilities are affected. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, including storm nights when several households are calling at once.

Pennypack Creek and the flood record

Pennypack Creek runs through the center of the borough, passing under PA 263, York Road. That puts the creek close to homes, shops and low-lying streets, and heavy rain can raise it quickly.

When the remnants of Ida crossed the region on September 1, 2021, the National Weather Service recorded flooding on Pennypack Creek at Davisville and Terwood Roads, and local reports said Hatboro declared a disaster emergency. A long, intense rain is the kind of event that tests basements and storm drains across the borough.

To see whether your property sits in a mapped flood zone, use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask the borough how floodplain rules affect repairs. Our flood damage cleanup page explains how contaminated flood water is handled, and the basement water damage page covers pumping and drying.

What the borough looks like on the ground

Hatboro covers 1.42 square miles and had 8,238 residents in 2020, with Upper Moreland Township on three sides and Warminster Township in Bucks County to the north. Moreland Avenue is the main east-west street, and the Warminster Line station and SEPTA Route 22 connect the center.

Older buildings sit close to the center. Loller Academy, built in 1811 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and the Union Library Company, founded in 1755, show how much of the downtown predates modern building materials. Older masonry and plaster dry more slowly, and crews adjust equipment and monitoring so historic finishes are not stressed.

Parks and open ground

Hatboro Memorial Pool and Park and Pennypack Park sit along the creek, which absorbs some runoff but also marks where water travels. Homes near those green spaces and the creek edge should check their basements first after a heavy rain.

What we handle in Hatboro

Flood and basement water lead the list here because of the creek. The storm water damage page covers rain that gets in through roofs and walls, and the sewage backup cleanup page covers backups that can follow a storm.

The borough shares a sewer authority with Upper Moreland, called the Upper Moreland-Hatboro Joint Sewer Authority. If a backup affects several neighbors at once, ask the authority whether the public line is involved. For homes in Jenkintown instead, see Jenkintown.

If water is still spreading

1

Shut off what you can

Close the main water valve for a pipe leak, and stay out of rising creek or storm-drain water.

2

Watch the electrical

Do not touch a wet panel, and call the utility or 911 about downed lines or gas smells.

3

Raise your belongings

Put valuables and electronics up high, and photograph the water line on walls.

4

Avoid contaminated water

Flood and sewer water can carry contamination, so keep children and pets away.

5

Call the 24/7 line

Say how deep the water is and where it entered, and ask what to do next.

How the estimate is set

The scope sets the price. The water category, the area, the materials, the drying days and the rebuild scope all matter, and flood water costs more to handle than clean water from a pipe. We explain the scope and the price before work starts.

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood and often exclude groundwater, so ask your insurer about flood coverage. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered. Read your policy before you assume anything.
