When water is spreading in Jenkintown

Water that is spreading through a Jenkintown home is an emergency, and the 24/7 line is open at any hour to take the call. Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is involved, keep people out of any room where water touches outlets, and tell dispatch which floor is wet and whether neighbors are affected.

Dispatch will ask the source, the depth and the materials that got wet. Emergency dispatch available around the clock means a leak found at midnight gets the same first conversation as a stain found at noon, with steps you can take right away.

A borough under a square mile, with Abington on three sides

Jenkintown is a borough of 0.59 square miles with 4,719 residents and 2,214 housing units counted in the 2020 Census. Abington Township wraps it on the north, west and east, and Cheltenham Township borders it on the south.

That density has a practical effect on water damage. A supply line failure in one building is close to the next, and in attached or multi-unit buildings water commonly crosses a ceiling or party wall into another home. Documenting which unit the water came from, and telling the owner or property manager in writing, makes the later conversation with insurers easier.

Our ceiling water damage repair page explains what happens when water drops from an upstairs unit, and the appliance leak water damage page covers the washer and dishwasher failures behind many multi-unit losses.

Roads, rail and getting crews to your door

The borough sits on PA 611, Old York Road, and its commercial blocks run along Greenwood Avenue and Washington Lane, which carries PA 73. The Jenkintown-Wyncote SEPTA station anchors the center of town.

Tight streets and limited parking mean crews need a clear path for hoses, pumps and drying equipment. If you have a reserved space or loading access, tell dispatch, and move cars so equipment can reach the entrance. Nearby shops along the main corridors are covered on our commercial water damage page.

Runoff, creeks and basements

The borough says its stormwater runoff drains to Tookany Creek and Pennypack Creek. For a homeowner, that means heavy rain can send water toward low spots and basements, and storm drains can back up when they fill.

Check your property on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask the borough about its floodplain rules. When water arrives through a basement wall or floor, the basement water damage page explains pumping and drying, and the flood damage cleanup page covers rising water.

Permits and the borough office

Jenkintown's own website says permits are required before work begins, and that plumbing permits go through master licensed plumbers. It lists the Borough Office at 700 Summit Avenue, (215) 885-0700, and says residential permits are decided within 15 business days.

Ask the borough what your repair needs before demolition or rebuilding. Mitigation, which is extraction and drying, usually starts right away, and permit questions then apply to the rebuild.

If water is still spreading

1

Stop the source

Close the main shutoff or the fixture valve, and note which unit or floor the water came from.

2

Protect the electrical

Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry, and call the utility or 911 about live wires.

3

Lift and move

Take rugs, electronics and papers off wet floors, and photograph the damage first.

4

Keep people clear

Avoid water that may be contaminated, especially if it came from a drain or a sewer.

5

Call the 24/7 line

Describe the source, the depth and the rooms, and ask what to do until the crew starts.

6

Tell your landlord or HOA

In a multi-unit building, report the leak in writing and keep a copy.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Jenkintown?

Is emergency water removal available in Jenkintown?

Do I need a permit to repair water damage in Jenkintown?

The borough says permits are required before work, so ask the Borough Office at 700 Summit Avenue. Mitigation like water removal and drying is a separate step from the rebuild.

Water came through my ceiling from the unit above. What now?
