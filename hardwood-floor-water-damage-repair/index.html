Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Abington, PA

Wood floors take water in slowly and show the damage days later. Call the 24/7 line early to dry hardwood before it cups, buckles or needs replacing.

Why hardwood needs fast action

Hardwood absorbs water through the edges and underside, so the damage often appears after the visible water is gone. A washer valve that broke and ran for an hour, or a condensation line that backed up behind a wall, can leave a floor that looks fine on day one and cupped on day four.

Wood swells as it takes up moisture and shrinks as it dries. Dry it slowly and early and many floors recover. Wait and the boards can lift permanently from the subfloor.

If the floor is wet now, stop the source, remove wet rugs and furniture, and photograph the floor before anything changes.

Cupping, crowning and buckling

Cupping

Board edges rise higher than the center, usually because moisture came from below.

Crowning

The center rises above the edges, often after a cupped floor was sanded before it dried.

Buckling

Boards lift off the subfloor, which usually means serious water and may require replacement.

Gapping

Boards shrink and gaps open as they dry, which may settle or need repair.

Cupping is the most common sign after a leak. Callers report walnut and oak floors that have started to cup after a nearby leak, and the key is to find the moisture source and dry the floor before sanding or refinishing.

How wood floors are dried

Drying hardwood is slower and more careful than drying drywall. Crews use specialized mats or panels that pull moisture out through the surface, and sometimes drill small holes along the edge or under cabinets to push air beneath the boards.

  1. 1

    Stop the source

    The leak is fixed, and standing water is extracted.

  2. 2

    Measure

    Moisture meters read the boards and the subfloor, compared with a dry area.

  3. 3

    Dry with controlled airflow

    Drying mats, air movers and dehumidifiers pull moisture out slowly.

  4. 4

    Recheck

    Readings are taken until the floor and subfloor reach the dry standard.

  5. 5

    Decide

    The floor is refinished, repaired in sections or replaced if the boards cannot recover.

Our structural drying page explains the equipment and the readings, and the appliance leak water damage page covers washer and dishwasher failures that often wet hardwood.

Repair or replace after drying

Repair starts only after the floor and subfloor are dry. Many floors flatten out as they dry, and sanding and refinishing can remove minor cupping and stains.

Why mold can follow a wet floor

Wood that stays wet under a floor or rug can grow mold, which the mold remediation page explains in detail. A musty smell or dark staining at the seams after a leak is a reason to call early.

What affects the estimate

The price depends on the flooring type, how many square feet got wet, whether the subfloor is wet too, how many days of drying are needed and how much repair or refinishing follows. Solid hardwood costs more to dry carefully but can often be saved.

We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to a real number. Sudden leaks are often covered by homeowners insurance, while long slow leaks may not be. Read your policy, ask your insurer and keep photos.

Hardwood in older homes

Older Abington homes often have wood floors laid over plank subfloors, which gives water more places to travel. The boroughs nearby have similar housing, and the pages on Jenkintown and Hatboro describe our coverage there.

What to do and what not to do

Remove standing water and wet rugs quickly, and keep foot traffic off swollen boards. Do not apply heat, such as a space heater or hair dryer, to force-dry the floor.

  • Do not seal wet wood with a finish or covering.
  • Lift furniture legs off the floor and use foil or plastic under them.
  • Photograph cupping or buckling the day you see it.
  • Call early, because boards that stay wet can be harder to save.

Drying wood floors is controlled work, and the method follows ANSI/IICRC S500.

Questions about hardwood floor water damage

Can a cupped hardwood floor be saved?

Often, if it is dried slowly and early. Many floors flatten as moisture leaves, and refinishing can follow once readings are dry.

Should I sand the floor right away?

No. Sanding wet wood can cause crowning and shrinkage problems. Wait until moisture readings show the boards and subfloor are dry.

How long does it take to dry a wood floor?

Often several days to a couple of weeks, depending on the species, thickness and how wet the subfloor got.

Is laminate flooring treated the same way?

Laminate and engineered floors usually absorb water at the seams and rarely recover, so replacement is common.
