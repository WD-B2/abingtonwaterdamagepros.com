A burst pipe is an emergency: what to do first

A burst pipe is an emergency because a pressurized supply line keeps running until someone shuts it off. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call as soon as the water is under control. The first job, though, is yours, and it takes under a minute.

Find the main shutoff valve, usually where the water line enters the house near the meter or in the basement, and turn it fully clockwise. Then open a low faucet to drain the remaining pressure out of the lines.

If water is dripping through a light fixture or near outlets, leave the area and call the electric utility or 911 before you call us. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it on dry floor.

Where the water goes after a pipe fails

Supply lines run inside walls, above ceilings and under floors, so a burst pipe rarely wets just one spot. Water follows framing and drops down through gaps, which is why a break in a second-floor wall can stain a first-floor ceiling.

Pressurized water also moves fast. A pipe that runs for an hour can wet insulation, drywall, trim and flooring across several rooms, and a pipe that runs while no one is home can reach every floor.

Because the water is usually clean at the start, damage from a burst pipe is often easier to dry than flood or sewage damage. That window closes with time, since standing clean water picks up contamination from building materials and dust.

Burst pipe or something else

Burst pipe

A supply line splits or a joint fails under pressure, water flows until the main is shut off, and the damage often spreads through walls.

Frozen pipe

Ice blocks a line and the split shows when it thaws, which has its own page because the cause and prevention differ.

Slab leak

The line runs under a concrete floor and leaks slowly, so symptoms are warm floor spots and a rising water bill.

Water heater leak

The tank or its fittings fail, and the water often comes from one fixed location.

Our frozen pipe and slab leak pages cover the two sources that behave most differently from a plain burst line.

What a crew does after a pipe break

  1. 1

    Confirm the water is off

    The crew checks the shutoff worked and the lines are not still leaking.

  2. 2

    Extract and remove

    Pumps and wet vacuums clear the floor, and soaked insulation or pad is removed.

  3. 3

    Open and map

    Meters and thermal imaging find water inside walls and ceilings, and small openings are cut where needed.

  4. 4

    Dry to standard

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference materials.

  5. 5

    Hand off to the plumber

    A licensed plumber repairs the pipe, and the rebuild starts once the structure is dry.

The plumber and the restoration crew do different work. Plumbing repairs and replacements often need permits, and the Abington Township Community Development Department at 1176 Old York Road can tell you which ones apply to your repair.

What affects the cost of burst pipe cleanup

Burst pipe cleanup is priced by what got wet. A single wall cavity near the break costs less than water that ran through a ceiling and two floors.

The main factors are how long the water ran, the number of rooms and floors affected, the materials involved, how many days of drying the structure needs, and how much rebuilding follows. We explain the scope and the price before work starts, and a call is the quickest way to get a real number for your property.

For insurance, sudden and accidental pipe failures are often covered while slow leaks often are not, so check your policy. Photograph the damage before cleanup and keep every receipt, as the Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises.

When a pipe bursts above a finished ceiling

Many burst pipe calls come in as a stained or sagging ceiling. In that case the visible damage is only the lowest point, and the real wet area sits above it. Our ceiling water damage repair page explains how crews open and dry that space, and the water extraction page covers the pumping side when water is on the floor.

Questions about burst pipe water damage

Do you handle burst pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Abington?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Shut off the main water valve first, then call and describe where the water is going. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Should I call a plumber or a restoration company first?

Shut off the water yourself, then call both. A plumber repairs the pipe, and a restoration crew removes the water and dries the structure. Starting drying early limits the damage.

What if the burst pipe is under the kitchen sink?

Close the sink's stop valves or the main, and clear the cabinet. Water under sinks soaks cabinet bases and spreads along the floor, so extraction and drying usually include the toe-kick and subfloor.

Will my insurance cover a burst pipe?

Sudden and accidental pipe failures are often covered, but coverage depends on your policy. Ask your insurer, photograph the damage and keep receipts.
